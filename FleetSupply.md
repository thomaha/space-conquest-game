# Fleet fuel sharing and tankers

## Implementation

The fleet page offers a Share fleet fuel workbench. Select a receiving fleet, supplier ships, receiver ships, supply type and source. Supplies can come from ordinary cargo, compatible working fuel tanks or dedicated supply compartments. No particular ship role is required.

The implemented command works at an owned shared stationary site or existing verified velocity-matched coasting contact. A receiving fleet can select suppliers from other fleets at the same site or contact. Movement in atmosphere, active thrust itineraries and warp do not permit sharing. A paused local departure can receive supplies, but cannot supply other ships while it retains an unfinished journey.

Fuel remains recorded on individual ships. Main propulsion, electrical generation and drive reactor feed are separate destinations. Chemical quantities count the entire fuel and oxidizer mixture; both ingredients must exist in the correct ratio. Storage capacity and fuel compatibility remain enforced by the existing ship transfer rules.

### Distribution

- Receiver fill is a percentage of propulsion or compatible electrical tank capacity.
- Drive reactor feed targets enough selected feed for that percentage of a full main propellant tank. Reactor feed occupies normal cargo capacity.
- Suppliers and receivers must be distinct selections. Every selected ship must belong to the receiving fleet's owner; receivers must still belong to the selected fleet.
- Receivers are supplied in selection-list order and suppliers are used in selection-list order. This first version fills toward the selected target rather than equalizing all ships.
- Partial supply is permitted and the preview reports each remaining shortfall. A request with no available transfers cannot be submitted.
- The preview lists each transfer and each selected ship's remaining main propellant, electrical fuel, dedicated supply fuel and cargo mass.
- Preview calculations run outside the JavaFX application thread. An immutable request and the exact reviewed transfers are staged for the next simulation tick.
- Execution rebuilds the complete allocation against live state. Changed allocations reject the whole command without a partial transfer. Changes that leave the allocation and reserve checks valid do not require a new preview.

### Protected supplier reserves

- The selected propulsion reserve defaults to 25% of tank capacity. If a supplier starts below that floor, sharing cannot reduce its main propellant further.
- Suppliers retain 48 hours of essential and cargo electricity. The forecast uses current stationary illumination with an eclipse-first cycle or darkness for physical drift. It conservatively uses the supplier's original cargo load for this check.
- Drive reactor feed for the supplier's remaining propellant is protected using its preferred existing feed. If that feed is already insufficient, sharing cannot reduce it further.
- A drifting supplier must still pass its own remaining physical recovery and electrical arrival-reserve checks after donating. This does not authorize recovery or change its motion.
- Legacy blueprints with unknown electrical equipment cannot supply through the fleet command. They can still receive compatible propulsion supplies and use the existing individual emergency transfer controls.
- Pending rescue commitments cannot be disturbed. Active trade carriers cannot donate freight through this command; cancel their route first.

The percentage floor protects stored propellant, but it does not certify a new departure or a return journey. Check actual travel readiness after sharing. Manual sharing does not allocate toward a chosen destination. Separate explicit orders support automatic electrical and propulsion deliveries during sublight coasting as described below.

Save version 37 persists dedicated supply inventory, containment exposure, pending deliveries and their latest outcome. Older ships load with empty supply compartments and no scheduled order. No credits are charged and no supplies are generated by sharing. Existing cryogenic cargo power demand and deterioration still apply while fuel is carried as freight.

## Tanker equipment and design presets

Tankers use the existing cargo transport role. Their equipment gives them dedicated storage and pumping capacity; the role itself grants neither. The ship designer includes a tanker preset with researched propulsion and electrical source choices. A tanker can join a mixed fleet through the existing organization controls.

| Equipment | Supply capacity | Dry mass | Slots | Continuous power | Complexity |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Bulk RP-1 tank | 40,000 kg | 2,500 kg | 6 | 2 kW | 2 |
| Bulk cryogenic tank | 40,000 kg | 3,000 kg | 6 | 40 kW | 3 |
| Reactor supply magazine | 100 kg | 500 kg | 1 | 1 kW | 3 |
| Fleet supply pump | 1,000 kg/h | 750 kg | 2 | 10 kW | 2 |
| Service cargo hold | 1,000 kg | 300 kg | 2 | 2 kW rated cargo demand | 1 |

All coefficients are provisional. Dedicated supply equipment requires electricity research and still passes the existing slot, structural, power and manufacturing validation. Construction uses the existing material-backed ship bill and work calculation. The pump and containment power are conservatively captured as continuous essential demand even when storage is empty or no delivery is taking place.

The RP-1 tank accepts kerosene. A cryogenic compartment shares its capacity between liquid oxygen, liquid methane, liquid hydrogen, hydrogen gas, deuterium gas and methane ice. The reactor magazine shares capacity between refined uranium, refined thorium and fusion pellets. Repeated modules add capacity within their compatible compartment type. Ordinary goods and antimatter are not accepted in these compartments.

Supply inventory is separate from ordinary cargo, main propellant and electrical generator fuel. New tanks are empty. The ship card buys individual material stocks from the current surface or dock hub; orbital loading pays an available lift provider. Purchases require owner cash, physical hub inventory and compatible free capacity. Ship copies retain supplies through movement, repairs, combat, passenger changes, ownership changes and working-tank refueling.

Loaded supply mass contributes to local maneuver fuel, launch payload, interstellar acceleration, peak speed, rescue and recovery. Design launch capability includes the maximum supply capacity. A tanker does not travel at an unloaded speed while carrying fuel for its fleet.

The preset replaces the ordinary cargo vault with compatible supply modules and a pump. Nuclear propulsion presets include a service hold for their own carried drive reactor feed and a supply magazine. Editing a registered tanker retains its supply equipment. An ordinary freighter can still carry fuel as cargo and share it without dedicated supply tanks. Hybrid layouts can combine the shared catalog's cargo and supply modules through blueprint specifications.

Liquid oxygen and methane use the existing six-hour outage grace and 2% daily boil-off rule; liquid hydrogen uses six hours and 3%. Boil-off follows equivalent essential-load outage hours and has its own persisted exposure and loss history. Working tanks are not affected. Gas and solid supply materials currently have no deterioration rule.

## Combined supply schedules during journeys

Configure suppliers in idle system space before departure. Select another member of the same fleet, compatible supplies, total mixture quantity and delay in hours after the original coasting phase begins. A fleet can schedule up to 64 deliveries. Orders capture ownership and the fleet's member IDs and reserve their combined dedicated supply stock. Each supplier's pumping intervals must not overlap. Manual withdrawals and bulk purchases on that supplier require cancelling its pending orders first. Ship cards can cancel individual deliveries or the supplier's entire schedule.

- Electrical deliveries replenish chemical auxiliary generators or electrical fission reactor stores. Propulsion deliveries refill the selected main drive's propellant tank. Drive reactor deliveries put the originally selected feed into normal cargo. All three destinations can share a voyage and every delivery replaces the remaining coast and braking plan.
- Automatic deliveries support physical sublight fleets with recognized chemical, MPD, fission or fusion main drives. Electrical fission generators are supported. Each new physical itinerary saves its selected drive, effective exhaust velocity, reactor feed and committed feed quantity by ship ID. Braking uses this saved exhaust even after the feed has been consumed. Older nuclear journeys without saved propulsion reject scheduled delivery rather than guessing a feed.
- Orders require direct departure from system space. Local travel, warp, manually replaced drift recovery and rescue trajectories require cancelling the order. After arrival, configure a fresh order for another voyage.
- Completion occurs at the original acceleration-end time plus the chosen delay plus quantity divided by the supplier's pump rate. This absolute voyage clock does not shift after replanning. The entire pumping interval must fit before braking begins. Simultaneous completions process reactor feed first, then use supplier and receiver IDs for deterministic ordering.
- The departure forecast processes acceleration, coasting, every delivery event, braking, the rest of the arrival day and the 48-hour essential and cargo reserve. Every member must reach its delivery using its own working stores. A delivery cannot mask an earlier outage.
- The same bounded phase calculations run in actual electrical ticks. Daily boundaries do not skip a short coasting delivery window. Supplies become usable at pumping completion and are transferred once.
- Transfers conserve material quantities and verify receiver capacity, remaining supplier stock and physical equipment. Each member's post-transfer loaded mass must support the remaining powered brake. Before committing a transfer, the forecast checks later scheduled deliveries and electrical endurance through arrival and its reserve. Changed future stock can therefore reject an earlier transfer atomically. Failure cancels all remaining fleet orders without transferring their stock.
- The supplier's own working propellant and generator fuel remain separate. Departure readiness checks its full journey and arrival reserve as well as the recipient's. Reserved quantities cannot exceed the supplier's actual bulk stock.
- Fleet splits, merges, member replacement, ownership changes and interrupted or replacement itineraries cancel the captured order during tick reconciliation while retaining physical supply stock. A power failure before delivery cancels the order without transferring remotely.
- Save/load retains pending deliveries and their latest completion, cancellation or failure explanation. Ship cards display reserved quantities and the last result.
- Scheduled departure previews run off the JavaFX application thread. A changed destination invalidates any earlier asynchronous preview result. Commands still revalidate the live tick state before departure.

Saved propulsion follows movement, power interruption, save/load and fleet split/merge. Replacement recovery and rescue plans capture their new feed choices. Arrival clears the completed propulsion plan. Added electrical fuel or reactor feed changes loaded mass and uses a newly calculated remaining brake rather than a fixed original braking budget.

The integration tests include a voyage rejected without scheduled supplies and completed with them, a delivery entirely inside one day, late or unreachable delivery rejection, mass and braking rejection, changed payload, outage, fleet split and save/load continuation.

## Scheduled propulsion supply and remaining-journey planning

Select propellant in the same coasting supply controls. The fuel choice identifies the recipient's main drive and quantity means total propellant mixture mass. Chemical delivery withdraws both fuel and oxidizer in that drive's captured ratio. MPD delivery uses methane ice and nuclear thermal or fusion delivery uses hydrogen reaction mass. Only compatible bulk compartments can supply these materials.

- Several propulsion deliveries can share the voyage with electrical and drive reactor supplies. Every ship must reach each delivery using its available working stores; later supplies cannot cover an earlier outage.
- The initial itinerary must be physically possible using the ships' working propellant. Refilling provides additional reserves; it does not authorize a departure that cannot reach coasting under its own power.
- Pumping must finish during the original coasting window. Materials stay with the supplier until completion. The recipient must have room at that moment, after its actual acceleration burn. Empty space at scheduling time is not required.
- At delivery, power and motion advance to the exact contact time. Every ship's actual position, shared velocity and remaining working fuel are retained. The supplier loses physical stock and the recipient gains that mixture once.
- The replacement plan keeps the fleet's current speed. It recomputes loaded mass and the slowest member's available acceleration, then schedules the remaining coast and a powered brake to zero speed at the original destination. This first version adds no acceleration burn after refilling.
- Each replacement braking budget is calculated from the saved effective exhaust velocity and post-transfer mass. Insufficient working propellant or insufficient remaining distance to brake rejects the entire transfer.
- Fission and fusion keep their original selected reactor feed and effective exhaust velocity. The unused share of already committed feed can fund the new brake. Any additional feed is withdrawn from that ship's real carried cargo. Tanker feed deliveries must match that original selection and fit normal cargo capacity. Missing feed rejects the transfer and deliveries cannot upgrade exhaust velocity in flight. Unused prepaid feed is not refunded as physical cargo.
- Before committing, the replacement plan and remaining supply schedule must fund every member's essential services, cargo preservation and active drive load through the arrival day plus 48 hours of arrival reserve. A failed stock, capacity, geometry, feed or electrical check keeps the current itinerary and transfers nothing.
- The tick processor and departure preview use bounded analytic phases rather than iterating through voyage days. A delivery inside a tick advances the old motion to contact and the new motion through the rest of that tick. Main propellant is not burned twice by the fleet processor.
- Cancellation before contact preserves paid motion and all remaining stock. Power failure or changed membership cancels the reservation. Pending orders, partial motion, delivery outcomes and replacement braking budgets survive save/load in version 37. Older orders without a destination default to electrical supply.
- Departure previews show the revised total arrival estimate and identify the original propellant budget separately. Successful delivery reports that the remaining coast and brake were replanned.

Tests compare preview and actual arrival, physical ingredient conservation and main-fuel accounting. They cover each chemical mixture, MPD electrical propulsion, fission and fusion feed commitments, pending and completed save/load, cancellation, depleted stock, outages, tank capacity and deliveries too late for safe braking.

## Destination and return reserves

### Agreed travel policy

Most civilian travel is expected to be from base to base. Plan and fund the next leg through arrival at the actual trading port, including controlled braking, local approach and an electrical waiting reserve. A trade ship may sell its cargo before choosing what to buy and where to travel next. Return planning is available but optional; an unfunded return does not reject a funded outbound leg.

Destination resupply must use compatible physical port stock. Current stock is information, not a promise that goods will remain available when a ship arrives. Purchases still require funds, capacity and local access. AI should prefer profitable destinations with compatible supplies and wait at a safe port when the next leg cannot be funded. Tanker rescue is assistance rather than an assumed substitute for departure readiness.

Propellant exhaustion means no thrust: no acceleration to increase speed and no powered braking to reduce it. There is no fuel-free low-speed travel mode. A moving ship keeps its actual ballistic velocity until a funded recovery maneuver changes it. A stationary ship remains stationary. Electricity independently gates powered propulsion. Physical local main propellant is consumed only during powered phases; drive reactor feed is committed once for each planned itinerary. Older prepaid local commitments remain paid.

Departure previews show each ship's predicted main propellant, electrical fuel and bulk supplies at the destination. The return check plans an immediate direct trip to the departure system using those working stores and carried drive reactor feed. It checks return electricity and a further 48-hour arrival reserve. A funded outbound journey does not automatically promise a funded return.

This cosmetic calculation runs outside the JavaFX application thread and never commits fuel or motion. It assumes no purchases, bulk transfers or dwell time at the destination. Passenger food and destination operations require separate planning. Local departures, warp and unrecognized legacy propulsion do not receive a physical return certification.

### Current trade implementation

- Known space-to-space journeys use frozen representative distance and the lowest loaded acceleration with fuel-supported acceleration, coasting and braking. Arrival scheduling rounds up to daily ticks. Zero usable thrust prevents departure and the complete trip must fund electricity, eclipses and a 48-hour arrival reserve. Surface transfers and legacy journeys retain their abstract phase rules.
- Local electrical forecasts repeat stable daily sunlight budgets analytically across actual burn and coast phases. Tick accounting consumes real reserves and burns main propellant only during powered phases. Paid electrical purchases add mass and trigger bounded duration rechecks. Interrupted physical local trips preserve ballistic motion and need a new funded recovery; older prepaid journeys preserve their existing progress. Save version 39 retains physical local state.
- Automated intersystem trade checks the departure maneuver, crossing, destination approach and 48-hour electrical port reserve as one next-leg budget. Crossing plans retain approach propellant instead of exhausting the working tank before docking. Nuclear approach feed must remain physically aboard.
- Market selection excludes intersystem legs that cannot currently be funded and prefers compatible stocked ports among profitable choices. Loaded cargo is checked again before purchase and launch are committed. Unsafe departures wait without paying for an unusable shipment.
- When stores are insufficient, automation buys compatible propulsion and electrical fuel at its current physical port using real stock and cash. Purchases seek available tank fill with bounded quantity searches and roll back if the complete leg remains unsafe. A local maneuver is rechecked after electrical fuel adds mass. Resupply costs enter the route's operating result.
- Port reports aggregate working-tank refill ingredients and selected reactor feed across fleet members. Full tanks still probe a small compatible supply quantity. Reports do not certify future inventory, launch throughput or affordability and do not buy remotely.
- Trade route cards calculate next-leg status asynchronously and explain missing port access, propellant, reactor feed, electricity or approach reserves. Fixed-origin recurring routes return to their configured origin. Optional roaming traders remain at the destination after selling and choose their next purchase and port on a later tick.
- Physical sublight and replacement trajectories stop shared thrust at the first member's actual propellant exhaustion time, retain position and velocity and drift without claiming controlled arrival. Coasting requires no propellant until a powered burn is needed. Scheduled deliveries cancel when the fleet cannot reach contact under its funded plan.

Trade departure checks use the conservative local solar envelope across actual acceleration, coasting and braking. They assume no destination purchases before approach. A provisional three-leg allocation retains real main fuel for the crossing and approach rather than spending the entire tank during departure. Every leg must still fund electricity and controlled braking. Booked passenger orders also check species supplies for their actual scheduled duration as described below. Legacy blueprints without recognized propulsion retain their existing compatibility behavior. See [LocalTravel.md](LocalTravel.md).

## Passenger journey supplies

- Every booked passenger ship in a fleet must carry its own species-specific daily supplies for the issued movement order. Conscious humans need both food and oxygen aboard even when the source world has a breathable atmosphere. Other species use the same requirements as daily passenger consumption.
- Local orders require `ceil(localDays)` of supplies. Interstellar orders require the separately rounded local departure and crossing durations, or the forecast scheduled crossing duration when coasting deliveries replan it. The earlier fixed four-day local allowance has been removed. Cargo mass contributes to maneuver duration, so readiness uses the actual loaded plan.
- A manual interstellar order ends in destination system space. A later docking or landing order has its own duration and supply check. Unissued approach legs, dwell time and a return trip are not silently added to the current order. Automated full passenger itineraries and optional nutritional waiting reserves remain future work.
- Checked supplies stay physically aboard and are consumed by the daily tick, including the final travel day before disembarkation. Preview and command submission do not prepay or remove food. Another ship's stock is not automatically pooled and no future purchase or rescue is assumed.
- Passengers continue consuming while travel is interrupted. Recovery checks current stores against the remaining local and queued crossing days, remaining warp days or a replacement physical trajectory rounded up to daily ticks. Earlier days are not charged again. Recovery still independently requires propulsion and electrical readiness.
- Valid cryogenic stasis consumes no nutritional supplies under the current provisional model. It requires the owner's research and enough equipped pod capacity, while existing electrical checks still apply. Unknown species, inconsistent passenger manifests, invalid durations and nonfinite required stock reject readiness.
- Existing cargo and passenger manifests preserve remaining supplies and headcounts through save/load. No save version change is needed. Long local travel, local and warp resumption, physical recovery and recovery UI blockers have regression coverage.

## Adaptive and mixed trade shipments

- Local departures and fixed-route empty returns use the same complete next-port readiness check. A non-null physical trajectory alone does not establish electrical endurance; a nearly empty tank can produce an impractically long coast.
- A safe physical local leg taking multiple scheduled days can preview a paid main-tank top-up and any needed electrical supplies. Commit this optional purchase only if it reduces scheduled travel days and the loaded leg still passes its arrival reserve. Missing stock or funds preserve the already safe slower departure. One-day legs skip this optional top-up.
- Main top-ups provisionally buy the largest affordable amount up to capacity. Purchases remain at the current physical port and are charged to the route, with unused supplies retained aboard. Carried surplus is useful insurance for unexpected maneuvers, diversions and combat, especially during war. Purchase policy should balance contingency reserves, cash, tank capacity and loaded mass; buying the absolute minimum is not the default goal. Saved fleet policies now protect configurable capacity-based targets for routine travel, cold war and total war. The live rules and tuning limits are in [ContingencyFuel.md](ContingencyFuel.md). No return itinerary is required for a next-port purchase.

- A trader can carry several goods to the same destination on one journey. The configured kg/load limit applies to their combined mass. Cargo capacity, passenger occupancy, launch access, current buying funds, buyer cash and destination storage are shared limits; source inventory thresholds and destination material stock ceilings apply per good.
- Both fixed-origin and roaming traders consider mixed baskets. The provisional planner sorts goods by current wholesale margin per kg with material ID tie-breaking and fills the shared budget. It compares that basket with single-good candidates using the complete loaded voyage's fuel, electrical, launch and tariff costs. Roaming traders continue to prefer stocked destinations and then expected profit per travel day.
- If the planned quantity is unsafe or unaffordable, the planner tries up to 12 successive halvings and eight refinements around a feasible quantity. It retains a profitable tested candidate without changing the configured load limit. Fixed routes retain their existing full-load behavior when it is funded; smaller fallbacks and mixed baskets require a positive expected margin. This bounded heuristic is provisional and does not guarantee a globally optimal mix or quantity.
- Every trial starts from the same immutable snapshot and buys all basket goods before checking the shared loaded journey. Only the selected snapshot commits. Failed departure or launch checks roll back cargo purchases as well as attempted resupply. Buyer stock, prices and cash can still change during travel; current demand is not a reservation.
- Orbital pickups buy a single physical cargo launch for the basket and allocate its paid cost by mass. Surface carrier launches and compatible working-fuel purchases remain part of the shared departure checks.
- A saved manifest tracks each material's remaining quantity and purchase or cargo-launch cost. Sales settle each material separately against the receiving port's remaining cash and storage. Partial sales preserve unsold lots and their cost basis. The trader waits there until every lot is sold or written off before choosing another port or returning.
- Lost freight writes off only the affected material's paid cost and never counts as a delivery. Unrelated ship cargo stays outside the trade manifest. Cancellation finishes the carried shipment and preserves every lot through daily financial copies.
- Save version 38 persists mixed manifests. Older single-good routes reconstruct their manifest from the existing material, onboard mass and cost fields. Route rows display quantities for all carried goods.
- The [trade reliability audit](TradeReliabilityReport.md) includes repeated mixed shipments. The [catalog trade and production audit](TradePropulsionReport.md) adds paid refinery output, production pauses, nuclear electricity, MPD propulsion and one-light-year departure probes. These fixtures do not certify the complete economy or every ship design.

## Trade reliability and port upkeep

- Active traders at a physical trading port check a provisional 60-day stationary essential and cargo electricity target even when no profitable shipment is available. The forecast includes local sunlight, eclipses, battery limits and existing generator stores. The separate journey arrival requirement remains 48 hours.
- If the target is not covered, upkeep buys compatible electrical feed with a 5% energy margin, limited by compartment capacity. It first tries the selected chemical mixture then checks the updated reserves before trying the selected reactor feed on ships with an installed reactor. It does not switch to an incompatible mixture or borrow main propulsion fuel, tanker supplies or freight.
- Purchases use real port stock and owner funds. When the complete requested top-up is unavailable, a bounded search buys the largest tested affordable partial quantity. Only the chosen purchase commits and its cost enters the route's operating result. Purchases do not start a journey or relax departure safety checks. Main propulsion contingency policies remain protected.
- The target is insurance rather than a guarantee. Insufficient output, tank capacity, funds or physical stock can still cause stationary outages. Docking does not provide free electricity. The 60-day coefficient is provisional and is not a saved per-fleet setting.
- Routes that dispatch move behind waiting routes in the next tick's processing order. This gives competing traders turns at replenished stock. The order uses the existing saved route list and needs no new save version.
- [TradeReliabilityReport.md](TradeReliabilityReport.md) records nine deterministic 360-day scenarios covering fixed and roaming traders, mixed loads, fuel shortages, price changes, buyer cash shortages and competing ships. It separates interrupted journeys from stationary electrical outages and expected consumption profit from actual refueling cash payments.
- The audit injects production, consumption and buyer liquidity and uses short physical crossings. Full economy feedback, realistic stellar distances and other propulsion types still need broader tuning.

### Trader departure dwell insurance

- A departure from a physical trading port must carry enough electricity for a 60-day stationary wait after the complete loaded journey when the destination's current compatible refill stock is insufficient or total scheduled travel exceeds two days. Travel duration includes each local leg, crossing and destination approach, rounded to daily ticks per leg. Fixed shipments, roaming shipments and empty returns use the same checks. No return itinerary is required.
- Short trips to currently stocked destinations retain the normal 48-hour arrival requirement. The shortage signal uses the existing fleet port refill probe against projected arrival stores, including propulsion and enabled electrical compartments. It does not reserve destination stock or predict its future production, prices or cash. A full-refill shortage can therefore require dwell insurance even where a smaller refill would be available.
- The departure forecast consumes actual phase-dependent electrical loads and propulsion before checking destination sunlight, eclipses, battery limits and essential and cargo demand. Each fleet member must cover its own wait; fleet totals cannot mask one ship's shortage. Explicit legacy electrical compatibility remains supported.
- Current-port purchases can fund the complete requirement. Rejected cargo and departure trials roll back attempted purchases; a route waits when no safe affordable shipment can be prepared. Source maintenance purchases remain separately paid upkeep. Destination purchases, cargo sale proceeds, tankers and main propulsion contingency fuel are not used to fund the carried electrical allowance.
- A shortage discovered after departure does not block a funded approach from system space. Existing journeys keep their committed motion and their normal physical and 48-hour arrival checks. New port departures reassess live conditions; no new save field is required.
- Queued crossing plans use the same post-departure-maneuver electrical and propulsion stores as previews. Planning a crossing with pre-maneuver electrical mass can prolong a fuel-limited crossing and invalidate its forecast endurance; the tick-owned queue now uses the consumed projection.
- Sudden shortages on short stocked trips, waits beyond 60 days, passengers' stationary nutrition, combat and changing markets remain tuning limits. Coefficients may change after broader economy and realistic-distance audits.

## Optional roaming trade

The trade workbench offers a roaming mode. It uses the selected origin as its initial trading port; no destination or return itinerary is required when commissioning it. The default remains a fixed-origin route with its existing market selection and return behavior.

- A roaming trader evaluates current source stock and destination demand after selling its previous shipment. It selects a new material and destination without returning empty to its original base.
- Each candidate simulates the paid cargo purchase, compatible local resupply, launch and loaded departure on an isolated immutable snapshot. Only the selected candidate commits on the trade tick. Rejected candidates leave stock, cash and ship state unchanged.
- Candidate quantity respects the shipment limit, source threshold, cargo capacity, buyer storage, posted demand, current buyer cash and available purchase cash. Loaded next-leg checks cover the destination port approach and electrical reserve. No tanker rescue or future destination purchase is assumed.
- Expected sale uses the current wholesale bid and destination tariff. Expected costs include cargo purchase, launch and the replacement price of consumed propulsion mixtures, oxidizer, electrical generator fuel and drive reactor feed. Missing fuel price quotes reject the candidate instead of treating carried fuel as free.
- Among profitable candidates, stocked destinations are preferred. Candidates within the same supply category compete on expected profit per planned travel day. Travel time uses the actual local and crossing plans instead of a fixed intersystem estimate. Stable material and port ID ordering resolves ties.
- Expected fuel expense prices consumption. Actual paid tank top-ups can cost more because some purchased supplies remain aboard for later trips. The existing route cash results charge real purchases immediately; a positive expected trade margin does not promise a positive cash result on every refueling day.
- Sale completes before the next trade is chosen. Partial sales remain attached to their original shipment. Cancellation finishes already carried cargo and then stops new purchases at the receiving port. A trader with no affordable, safe and profitable shipment waits and reports that reason.
- Save version 37 persists roaming mode and the latest decision or waiting message. Older routes default to fixed-origin mode. Financial updates and cancellation retain the chosen mode.

Prices, stock and buyer funds can change during travel. Profit is an estimate and does not reserve the destination sale. Conservative local power costing assumes darkness; mission food, demand forecasting, optimal partial loads and broader route search tuning remain future refinements.

### Intermediate parking ports

If no direct profitable shipment exists, an empty single-ship roaming trader can consider one intermediate operational station on its current body. The bounded search examines up to eight ports ordered by altitude difference then ID and rejects access longer than thirty scheduled days. It projects funded arrival, remaining stores and campaign time before quoting a new direct trade from the depot. Combined expected profit subtracts consumed access fuel and launch cost before ranking profit per total scheduled day. Prepaid fuel still has a replacement cost.

Only supplies bought at the actual current port and the first parking departure commit. The saved `REPOSITIONING` phase has an empty cargo manifest and claims no delivered volume. After actual depot docking, the route adopts that port and selects its next shipment against live stock, prices and funds. No remote supplies or sale are reserved during lookahead. A cancelled route completes its committed stop without loading again and a failed burn reports interruption without awarding depot arrival. Passengers, carried cargo, surface access, other-body staging and longer intermediate chains are outside this first planner. See [StagedLunarTradeReport.md](StagedLunarTradeReport.md).

Save version 37 retains multiple pending orders and the original voyage clock across daily ticks and replacement braking plans. Older single orders remain supported and orders without a destination default to electrical supply. Pending version 35 recovery trajectories infer their original clock when resumed.

## Draft extensions

The next extensions are optional reacceleration after refill, automatic return allocations from bulk supplies, complete passenger itineraries and dwell reserves and supply policies shared across several voyages. Manual transfers remain instantaneous and lossless at allowed contact; pumping duration is enforced by scheduled deliveries. Rates, containment loads, capacity, spill losses and material-specific deterioration need further tuning.

## Orbital waiting and encounter limits

Orbital orders can buy real compatible fuel and electrical supplies or paid grid charging while still waiting at their source base. Escape rechecks complete remaining main and auxiliary budgets. Timed tanker rendezvous during orbital coast are unsupported and cannot transfer supplies merely when a timer expires. Missing capture reserves cause a missed encounter with continued gravitational coast. Return planning stays optional and unused maneuver allocations stay aboard. See [OrbitalTransfers.md](OrbitalTransfers.md).

Main-fuel and electrical controls remain available in the fleet UI during source waiting. Commands consume real market stock or stored grid electricity only when executed; port access ends after escape. [OrbitalTradeReport.md](OrbitalTradeReport.md) measures paid mixed voyages and distinguishes retained fuel inventory, route cargo margin and consumed-supply costs. Reproduce the report with `mvn test` on JDK 27; the generated copy is `engine/target/orbital-trade-report.md`.
