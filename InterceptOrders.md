# Fleet intercept orders

## Implemented behavior

An empire may explicitly assign an idle military fleet to intercept a currently detected hostile fleet in the same system. Both command execution and later ticks check actual installed sensor coverage and TOTAL_WAR diplomacy. Saved exploration or a previously detected contact does not authorize pursuit. Any owned fleet or operational station with online sensor hardware may provide coverage.

The intercept fleet must contain live owned military hulls with owned blueprints and installed main drives. Guard, escort, passenger, active trade, supply delivery and approaching rescue commitments prevent assignment. No blueprint sharing is introduced.

Orders are staged through InterceptFleetCommand and the normal command queue. FleetInterceptProcessor runs before the daily fleet power and movement steps. A detected stationary target can trigger an ordinary local physical or orbital journey. Existing main fuel reserves, reactor feed, power forecasting, finite-burn limits and paid maneuvers still apply. A rejected departure leaves the order waiting without adding fuel or moving the fleet.

### Funded stand-off parking

FleetStandOffApproach tries a useful firing position when a stationary target and an idle hunter occupy known parking sites around the same planet or moon. FleetCombatPositioning reads surviving installed application ranges and quotes funded salvos, expected accuracy, projectile delay and threat reduction against potential return fire. It can preserve long-range safety or close for short-range weapons. Disabled mounts, destroyed ships and empty magazines do not supply candidate ranges. Doctrine tuning comes from combat_positioning_doctrine.json; weapon tuning remains in technological applications. This query grants no blueprint ownership.

At most 18 radial candidates use the existing native parking planner, with offsets on either side of the target altitude. Orbital phase matters: altitude difference alone is not firing separation. Cheap salvo samples rank candidate offsets while retaining a close option for each sampled mount range. Each physical candidate funds ordinary departure, capture and braking plus reactor feed, electrical upkeep and existing reserves. ArrivalPreview supplies projected stores, SensorPosition checks actual model separation at the coordinated arrival day and SensorCoverage validates shared targeting from the arrival snapshot. The candidate with the highest discounted arrival score is selected. Leaving an existing firing position requires the doctrine's minimum improvement after travel costs. This bounded search does not optimize all possible orbits. Planning consumes no resources.

The committed native itinerary retains its stand-off destination while the intercept order still names the hostile fleet. Real ticks pay its maneuvers and braking. After arrival, normal live contact and range rules permit engagement and ordinary intercept completion. Cancellation or lost contact clears intent without redirecting the already funded flight. Existing itinerary and order fields suffice at save version 62.

Unsupported sites, different bodies or rejected candidates retain the ordinary approach. A fleet already in reach holds when no improved position can be funded. Moving targets can use the body-coast approach below or retain their existing rendezvous planners. Parking stand-off neither holds a formation at a fixed distance nor adds continuous pursuit steering; the target's later movement can change the engagement.

FleetStandOffApproachTest checks the actual arrival separation of a laser ship outside cannon reach, positive capture fuel cost, preview and tick fuel agreement, dry-tank rejection, moving-target exclusion and cancellation with save/load preserving the paid itinerary.

### Moving stand-off around a body

BodyFleetStandOff supports detected planetary and lunar vector coasts, native orbital coasts and missed body captures within the existing observed-coast horizon. The hunter starts from known parking or an eligible interrupted body flight in the same gravity frame. FleetCombatPositioning supplies the preferred sampled firing offset using captured application capabilities and doctrine priorities. The arrival must retain a useful funded salvo. Both planners read installed weapon stats rather than selecting weapon family IDs; projectile and ammunition rules remain authoritative.

At most 14 candidates try one through seven coordinated arrival days and radial or velocity-direction offsets from the predicted target position. The existing gravity shooting helper funds two finite burns and an intervening coast. It must match the observed target velocity within 0.0005 m/s, remain inside the body's physical and sphere-of-influence bounds, respect the slowest loaded ship's thrust and delta-v and fund travel power, main fuel, reactor feed, reserves and an arrival salvo. Bounded shooting can reject feasible low-orbit or long approaches; failure retains the existing rendezvous fallback. No new stellar or gravity-free stand-off planner is introduced.

The ordinary intercept order and saved vector trajectory retain the target ID and actual motion. Successful ranged contact is WEAPON_RANGE rather than physical rendezvous. Completed approach release preserves its actual position and velocity and continues gravity-driven coast; equal velocity at arrival does not guarantee a permanently fixed separation. Changed target motion can invalidate the predicted engagement. Cancellation and lost contact clear intent without refunding resources or redirecting the committed flight. Save version remains 62.

BodyFleetStandOffTest checks planetary and lunar vector and native coasts at actual arrival, reachable separation, velocity agreement, positive braking, preview and tick fuel agreement, ranged encounter history and retained physical state. Dry tanks cannot launch and cancellation, lost sensor coverage and save/load preserve stores and committed motion. These checks establish the mechanism rather than fleet balance or universal trajectory feasibility.

MovingFleetRendezvous can intercept a detected straight-line local coast or ballistic drift. The hunter starts at rest at a deep-space site. An on-corridor approach uses the analytic forward planner. VectorInterceptPlanner handles sideways starts using two finite burns and an intervening coast in three dimensions. It tries at most 48 geometrically spaced durations, checks loaded acceleration, actual vector delta-v, speed limits and electrical readiness then selects the first funded candidate. This bounded search can miss other feasible trajectories and does not optimize fuel or arrival time globally.

Both planners use the slowest loaded hull's capabilities, actual tank and reactor-feed reserves plus the current observed target position and velocity. For a controlled coast the meeting tick must finish before the observed coast ends; other observed drift is bounded to a 30-day planning horizon. Insufficient fuel or power, excessive target speed and short coast windows leave the order waiting.

OrbitalRendezvousPlanner adds detected stellar-frame Hohmann coasts and failed stellar captures. A hunter at rest in deep space may attempt one of seven coordinated meeting ticks, one through seven days away, before a controlled target's capture event. Each candidate predicts the observed ellipse's position and velocity and iteratively corrects two finite burn directions under the star's actual gravity. The shooting search performs at most twenty corrections per candidate and rejects unfunded, excessive-speed, inner-body or unconverged trajectories. It requires predicted position agreement within 0.05 m and velocity agreement within 0.00005 m/s before commitment. Candidate start and meeting radii below 0.1 AU are outside this first solver's domain.

TwoBodyFlight uses phase-separated RK4 integration with steps no longer than 120 seconds. Actual gravity motion advances from the saved position and velocity rather than restarting from a projected route point. After cancelled, missed or power-failed approaches, the ship follows gravity-driven ballistic motion. Gravity changes velocity without spending main propellant; only the actual thrust impulses consume funded propellant and reactor feed. Confirmed contact uses the same one-meter and 0.001 m/s live tolerances after both fleets advance.

A committed journey cannot be redirected mid-flight. At the end of a stationary-site journey, a still-detected target at another stationary site can trigger another funded leg. Sensor contact loss, target removal, departure from the system or peace ends pursuit. There is no automatic reacquisition after an order has ended.

The tactical encounter adapter gives the interceptor's named target priority when live sensor coverage and actual weapon reach permit combat. Provisional ranges are 100 km for cannons, 500 km for mass drivers and 1,000 km for lasers. Detection alone does not grant weapon reach. An idle interceptor already within reach holds position instead of starting another funded leg. Its order completes after stationary in-range encounter processing, physical site contact or target removal. Guard and escort remain separate explicit orders. See [CombatModules.md](CombatModules.md#weapon-engagement-ranges).

Moving rendezvous contact is checked after all fleets advance. The completed hunter continues at its matched velocity through the rest of the tick. Confirmed contact requires current sensor coverage, the same target corridor definition, three-dimensional position agreement within one meter and full velocity agreement within 0.001 m/s. It never claims docking or arrival at the target's eventual destination. A changed target trajectory, position or velocity prevents confirmed rendezvous but can leave the ships within weapon range. Firing uses live detection and actual separation independently of velocity matching. Successful and missed approaches end in actual drift with ordinary local recovery available when funded. Campaign battle history marks confirmed moving contact as RENDEZVOUS and earlier or unmatched ranged combat as WEAPON_RANGE. A committed approach continues physically during combat; entering range does not teleport, redirect or brake it.

VectorFlight preserves actual position, velocity and phase time during a sideways approach or subsequent drift. Propellant consumption follows the magnitudes of both actual burn vectors; a conservative full-power phase envelope drives the existing electricity forecast and tick. Sensor positions read the vector state directly. An interrupted burn drifts from its real position and velocity. RecoverFleetTravelCommand can fund a new vector trajectory from that state to the existing frozen local destination with zero terminal velocity, using the same 30-day bounded search. It does not project the ship onto the route before replanning.

## Cancellation and persistence

CancelInterceptFleetCommand removes onward pursuit without undoing the current trajectory, spent fuel or committed burns. Cancelling orbital travel also clears intercept intent and uses the existing physical cancellation rules. Manual movement, replacement stance, guard assignment and fleet reorganization replace intercept intent.

Save version 47 introduced FleetInterceptOrder on the immutable Fleet snapshot. Save version 48 added the rendezvous target marker. Save version 49 added optional VectorFlight trajectories, actual position, velocity and elapsed phase time. Save version 50 retains the optional central gravity descriptor; older vector trajectories default to no gravity. Ordinary power, movement, fleet copies and compatible formation updates retain physical vector state. Owner changes and interstellar departures clear intercept intent.

Fleet management offers detected hostile target choices and queues assignment or cancellation. It does not plan physical travel on the JavaFX application thread.

## Current limits

Stationary-site pursuit, straight-line coast or drift rendezvous and stellar, planetary and lunar curved coast rendezvous are implemented. Accelerating targets, cross-body combat approaches, remote attacks and surface attacks remain unsupported. These contacts wait for a supported opportunity under current coverage. Circular recovery supports interrupted VectorFlight and failed or canceled OrbitalFlight captures in stellar, planetary and lunar frames through one shared mechanism.

BodyFleetRendezvous starts from actual parking motion or an interrupted planetary or lunar coast around the same body. It uses the shared gravity shooting planner and tries up to 48 durations scaled from the local orbital period, bounded to seven days. The target must remain ballistic through the coordinated daily meeting tick, including any coast after an earlier subday rendezvous. Native Hohmann coasts, missed captures and vector coasts retain their actual position and velocity. Funded thrust consumes the ordinary protected fuel, reactor feed and electrical budgets. The planner rejects paths through the body or outside its sphere-of-influence bound. Equal named orbit sites are allowed only with an explicit gravity vector trajectory because orbit labels do not encode phase.

Live authorized shared sensor hardware can support body pursuit. Losing an agreement or the contributing hardware clears intent while a committed journey continues physically without refunds. Combat still requires current wartime coverage plus actual position agreement within one meter and velocity agreement within 0.001 m/s. CancelOrbitalTravelCommand clears intercept intent as it stops body-vector thrust. CancelInterceptFleetCommand clears intent while retaining already committed motion. BodyFleetRendezvousTest checks Earth and Moon vector and native coasts, paid tick and forecast agreement, persistence, interrupted starts, cancellation, short coast windows, frame mismatch, fuel or power failure, changed velocity and lost corporate coverage.

## Shared orbital recovery

Completed circular planetary or lunar recovery now connects to ordinary parking travel through RecoveredParkingTravel. Queued local movement can fund station docking or a same-body altitude transfer from actual position and velocity. Native parking events retain source phase, main fuel reserves, reactor feed, electricity and passenger checks. Failed or canceled approaches keep physical coast and free-orbit arrivals retain actual circular motion. Recovered docking now predicts station phase and matches velocity through the shared gravity-frame planner. A missed approach retains actual coast. New native arrivals also save and fund this physical approach through OrbitalDocking in save version 53. Older native records retain their original event semantics. See [ParkingOrbitTransfers.md](ParkingOrbitTransfers.md).

RecoverOrbitalTravelCommand lets an owned interrupted vector fleet or failed capture choose a prograde circular orbit by radius and current phase in its saved gravity frame. OrbitalRecovery uses actual relative position and velocity, the selected center's mass and physical radius plus an outer sphere-of-influence bound for bodies. The same shooting helper, paid thrust budgets and interruption behavior serve stellar, planetary and lunar recovery. StellarRecovery and RecoverStellarTravelCommand remain compatibility entry points restricted to stellar states.

A failed or canceled OrbitalFlight must have executed departure and retain an unexecuted capture or circularization. Waiting orders, active controlled coasts and already captured parking orbits do not qualify. Preview leaves the original itinerary intact; only funded command execution replaces its unexecuted events with the new vector trajectory. No old fuel is refunded and no old capture or approach can run afterwards. The shared command can require an expected frame so a queued action cannot silently change gravity centers.

The bounded search tries seven candidates with at most twenty shooting corrections each. Stellar durations are one through seven days and retain the 0.1 AU minimum domain. Body durations scale from one 128th of the target circular period by successive factors of 1.6, capped at seven days. Target and predicted path radii must stay above the central body and inside its sphere-of-influence approximation. Shooting starts from the unpowered gravity prediction. Loaded acceleration, main fuel contingency, reactor feed, electricity and passenger supplies must fund the result. Unfunded or unsupported choices leave state unchanged. Integration steps are capped at 120 seconds and one 2048th of the local circular period.

Completion matches position and orbital velocity and retains physical vector state for continued gravity-driven coast. Logical deep-space or parking destination labels do not grant docking or a fixed position. No additional main fuel is used after completed burns. CancelOrbitalTravelCommand stops vector thrust immediately without advancing the clock or changing saved position and velocity. Electrical failure likewise interrupts thrust while gravity continues. Recovery can then be replanned from the new actual state. SensorPosition converts body-relative vectors to system coordinates using the moving planet or moon center at the snapshot epoch.

The shared recovery card selects stellar radius in AU or body parking altitude in km and current phase in degrees. Previews run off the JavaFX thread and reject stale results; actions enqueue commands that recompute readiness at execution. Ordinary gravity-free recovery remains separate. Save version 51 persists the gravity frame and outer planning radius alongside the trajectory and actual relative state. Older gravity JSON defaults to a stellar frame without an outer bound.

Save version 52 adds an optional physical source coast to native parking itineraries. This lets ordinary parking travel preserve the completed recovery's actual phase while waiting or when an approach fails. Old native itineraries have no source coast and retain their previous semantics.

Gravity-free local flight and recovery retain the existing frozen destination geometry and approximate illumination model. Stellar integration models only the central star and inherits the current circular coplanar target ephemeris. It does not add planetary perturbations, collision damage or tactical weapon ranges. Planned paths entering the star are rejected. Unfunded drift uses a finite uniform-density interior potential if it crosses the stellar surface; no ships are deleted or awarded capture there. Stellar collision consequences need a separate game rule. The bounded shooting search can miss other reachable paths and does not claim an optimal interception.
