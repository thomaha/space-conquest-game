## Implemented mechanics

### Application-defined capabilities

Every installed weapon and shield has a technological application in `engine/src/main/resources/technologies.json`. The application's `combatModule` defines its slot use, mass, idle demand, complexity, construction materials and operational capabilities. Operational values include damage, range, salvo energy, defensive capacity, defensive shot energy, ammunition resource and packaged mass, projectile speed, launches per round, live-guidance requirement and shield recharge energy. `CombatModuleCatalog` loads these definitions and derives ownership gates from the parent technology and application prerequisites; it contains no weapon-specific numerical capabilities.

The cannon now requires developed `ship_cannons` under industrial production and the guided launcher requires developed `guided_missile_weapons` under rocketry. Opening era presets explicitly develop those applications to preserve their existing weapon availability. Older installed hardware remains usable; older empires must develop a missing application before registering a new design that uses it.

Each application's `effectStats` names the operational capabilities affected by its accepted performance modifier. New blueprints capture the optimized combat modules in an immutable `ShipDesign.combatModules` map. Cost and complexity follow the existing application production path. Combat, damage, idle power, ammunition loading, fleet cartridge counts, construction receipts and designer descriptions read those captured values or the same application definition. Research and later catalog changes do not upgrade already captured hardware. Ordinary shared combat rules such as simultaneous rounds, hull damage scaling and target assignment remain engine algorithms.

Save version 63 persists the captured combat modules. Saves without that field resolve their existing installed IDs through baseline application data once during loading. Unknown modules supply no invented weapons. Carrier wings and planetary batteries also name a weapon application and capture its module instead of accepting arbitrary firepower. Their saved finite stores and paid resupply are described under [Auxiliary weapon supplies](#auxiliary-weapon-supplies).

Ship firepower comes from installed combat modules rather than dry mass or military role. CombatModuleCatalog defines provisional ship cannons, pulse lasers, particle beams, ion weapons, mass drivers, guided missile launchers and shield emitters. Multiple installed mounts add their damage or shield capacity. Unknown components and missing blueprints grant no invented weapons or shields.

### Tactical target selection

The automatic tactical objective is to reduce the enemy fleet's incoming damage quickly. CombatTargetSelector scores living ships using currently funded offensive firepower at the encounter separation, immediate projected threat reduction and expected useful hits to remove offensive capability. Fast neutralization of a dangerous ship receives priority over finishing a nearly harmless target. A small data-defined focus multiplier encourages continued fire while a target remains useful. Each actual hit updates shields, hull and electronics before the next target choice, so destroyed or disarmed targets lose priority immediately.

Suitability follows captured technological applications rather than weapon names. Projections use WeaponImpactResolver and discarded ShipCombatPower quotes, including shield bypass, armor absorption, physical or electronic damage, range, target acceleration, ammunition and electrical supply. Ion can remove funded offense through electronics without hull damage; beams retain application-defined tracking and kinetic bypass can favor a shielded target. Empty magazines and disabled equipment contribute no current funded offense. Other reachable targets remain available when no enemy can currently fire. Quotes consume no resources and actual misses retain existing paid firing costs.

The weights and focus multiplier live in [combat_targeting_doctrine.json](engine/src/main/resources/combat_targeting_doctrine.json). Weapon balance values remain in technological applications and their immutable blueprint captures. The default forecast evaluates at most eight repeated successful impacts per candidate with accuracy-adjusted expected shot count, then extrapolates remaining work. The configurable projection depth has a safety ceiling of 32. This is a bounded heuristic over the current paid hit, not a guarantee of optimal mixed-salvo allocation. It does not predict reinforcement, repair, resupply, movement or future shield recharge. Projectiles retain existing fleet-level guidance and choose a surviving ship at impact.

ProjectileTargetingContext snapshots actual outstanding friendly and hostile flights after paid interception and flight advancement. It assigns provisional friendly impacts to discarded target snapshots, reducing additional fire priority when committed shots already remove a target's threat. Accuracy and arrival delay reduce confidence. Arriving projectiles reserve their expected damage before direct hits in the same volley, then leave that reservation before their own impact is selected. Every subsequent choice recalculates against actual surviving condition. Reservations spend nothing, grant no damage and do not lock a ship target. Intercepted, arrived, expired or guidance-lost flights leave the live flight list and cannot reserve future work.

Guided incoming damage adds threat to a critical contact provider even after its launcher's magazine is empty. The existing shared-contact query determines whether removing or disabling that ship actually breaks enemy guidance. Another ship, external fleet or shared station can keep contact alive; in that case the ship gains no critical-guidance threat. Applications define whether each projectile needs live guidance. Existing guidance-loss execution destroys only guided flights at the next guidance check and retains spent launch costs. Current-round incoming hits remain simultaneous.

Doctrine data adds committedFireWeight, arrivalDelayDiscount, guidanceThreatWeight and maxPendingImpacts. Reservation weight ranges from zero to one; delay and guidance weights are nonnegative. The default pending forecast considers the earliest 64 flights with a safety ceiling of 256. Guided risk uses delayed potential incoming damage; it does not assume future interception or perfect accuracy. Tests change local doctrine and application copies without rewriting production weapon values.

TARGET_SELECTION detailed actions explain funded weapon firepower per round separately from critical guidance threat, estimated neutralization hits, immediate expected threat reduction and committed-fire reduction. Single-target decisions and useful-damage fallbacks also produce explanations. Forecasts are explicitly estimates rather than guaranteed damage. Tactical playback displays these actions without a UI mutation path. Concentration changes target assignment without changing shot funding, interception, contact requirements or simultaneous enemy return fire in the current round.

Save version 71 adds a bounded trace to campaign history: the last 64 targeting decisions per encounter with round, target and explanation. The fleet panel displays up to three owned-participant encounters and the last twelve decisions in each. Older saves default to an empty trace and the panel states that explanations were not recorded. These historical estimates do not grant new live targeting or rerun combat forecasts.

CombatTargetSelectionTest compares an actual paid two-pulse salvo with rotating hits and verifies lower remaining next-round enemy firepower. It checks finishing and switching, dangerous-target priority, ion disablement, agile-target suitability, standing-shield bypass, empty magazines, range re-quotation, deterministic repeats and local application or doctrine edits. Run it with `mvn -q -pl engine test '-Dtest=CombatTargetSelectionTest'`. Production application balance values remain unchanged.

ProjectileCoordinationTest adds bounded near and distant reservations, current-volley projectile accounting, paid mixed beam and slug delivery, disablement before a slow projectile arrives, live critical-relay suppression and external shared coverage. WeaponApplicationFlightTest checks immutable pending snapshots through paid interception, actual arrival and lost guidance. Run these with `mvn -q -pl engine test '-Dtest=ProjectileCoordinationTest,WeaponApplicationFlightTest,CombatTargetSelectionTest'`.

### Fleet combat positioning

FleetCombatPositioning quotes the installed fleet's next funded salvo at a candidate separation. It uses captured technological applications for range, pulse count, ammunition, electrical demand, accuracy, target acceleration sensitivity, projectile speed, shield bypass, armor absorption and electronic damage. Discarded expected impacts share CombatTargetSelector's focus on neutralizing enemy offense and reevaluate projected targets between hits. Remaining enemy potential is quoted at close range so disabling a currently distant weapon still has value. Quotes never spend cargo, charge or fuel and grant no actual damage.

The position score values removed funded enemy offense and progress against shields, hull or electronics while penalizing potential simultaneous incoming damage. Projectile delay discounts expected work using captured speed; beams have immediate delivery. Return-fire risk remains conservative and does not promise future interception or guidance loss. It uses the original enemy snapshots because a successful disabling shot cannot cancel this round's already-funded return fire. This is a limited salvo forecast rather than a full battle or weapon balance simulation.

CombatPositioningDoctrine loads validated values from `engine/src/main/resources/combat_positioning_doctrine.json`. Data controls range fractions, neutralization and progress weights, incoming-damage and distance weights, arrival and travel discounts, the minimum improvement needed to leave an existing firing position and maxSalvoImpacts. Defaults sample nine fractions across at most nine distinct installed ranges, retaining nine physical offsets and a close option for each sampled range. Each quote projects at most 64 impacts per side; validation caps fractions at nine and impacts at 256. Weapon balance stays in application technologies and new construction captures those values without upgrading existing hardware.

Stationary pursuit evaluates up to 18 ordinary parking destinations around the same body, checks funded departure, braking and shared sensor coverage at arrival then ranks the surviving arrival salvos after travel costs. It can close for short-range mounts even when another mount already reaches the enemy. If repositioning cannot be funded, an already reachable fleet keeps its position. Moving pursuit derives a preferred offset from the same quote and retains the existing maximum of 14 coordinated gravity shots with target velocity matching. Unsupported geometry keeps ordinary rendezvous fallback. Committed journeys retain actual motion through cancellation and save/load.

Positions belong to whole fleets. Tactical rounds keep the encounter's physical geometry and individual ships do not gain free movement, independent steering or new saved position fields. Guards and escorts retain their existing mission and safety gates. A completed useful firing position releases ordinary pursuit intent rather than running a new travel search every round.

FleetCombatPositioningTest checks safe beam range, kinetic accuracy and speed, pulse-laser reach, electronic disablement, depleted or disabled hardware, immutable stores and local application and doctrine edits. FleetStandOffApproachTest adds paid kinetic and laser arrivals against ion support and mixed-mount closing. BodyFleetStandOffTest retains planetary and lunar arrival, matched velocity, paid braking, cancellation and saved motion checks. Run these with `mvn -q -pl control -am test '-Dtest=FleetCombatPositioningTest,FleetStandOffApproachTest,BodyFleetStandOffTest,InterceptFleetCommandTest' '-Dsurefire.failIfNoSpecifiedTests=false'`.

### Future balance tuning

Current values remain provisional. Balance weapons by editing their applications in `engine/src/main/resources/technologies.json`; consumers read these definitions and captured blueprint values. The limited tuning probe checks mechanics and data propagation rather than fleet win rates or final balance. No balance values were changed by this probe.

Damage is per salvo for immediate weapons and per launch for projectiles. Firing energy uses the same distinction. Immediate ammunition shots consume one packaged round per shot; projectile launch count multiplies per-launch damage, energy and ammunition. Keep packaged masses and manufacturing output consistent when changing round sizes.

Catalogs load at application startup. Restart after editing data and build fresh blueprints to evaluate new hardware. Saved blueprints preserve their captured capabilities, so editing application values does not rebalance existing captured weapons. Manufacturing recipes use the current loaded application data. There is no live reload or automatic retrofit. Fractions must stay between 0 and 1 and shot counts must be positive whole numbers. Shared engine rules such as the combat interval, hull condition and generic armor deflection remain separate from weapon-specific coefficients.

Each fired round consumes its full shot energy even when hull or mount damage reduces the damage delivered. Empty magazines, residual mass, disabled mounts and insufficient shot energy cannot fire. Cannon impacts retain their existing immediate tactical delivery; travel and interception remain application-defined capabilities.

ShipDirectWeapons plans immediate fire from captured application values. Energy-only salvos retain proportional funding before whole ammunition shots. ### Per-weapon delivery and impact

WeaponHit preserves each paid pulse or projectile with its frozen ShipModule. ShipCombatPower returns separate direct hits and projectile launches. GuidedMissileFlight retains the hit capabilities throughout tick-local travel. WeaponImpactResolver assigns hits across surviving targets, checks accuracy and applies shield absorption before armor and hull damage. A bypass share reaches armor while shields still protect against the rest; it never grants bypass to another weapon. Both fleets pay and prepare their fire before either fleet takes damage.

Mass drivers use medium-range physical projectiles at a provisional 25 km/s. Their data grants 35% shield bypass, a 0.7 hull-damage multiplier against armor at least 5 cm thick, 95% base accuracy and 40% relative accuracy loss at maximum range. The accuracy formula is base accuracy times one minus the declared range falloff times squared range fraction, divided by one plus target acceleration times the declared sensitivity. Target acceleration comes from surviving thrust and actual carried mass. Slugs have no electronics; laser or kinetic point defense can intercept them but ion point defense cannot. The current mass-driver application retains its earlier ammunition-free abstraction; finite slug manufacturing and cargo remain a separate mechanic.

Particle beams use long-range immediate delivery, perfect accuracy and no shield bypass. They apply normal physical damage after melting shields and cannot enter the projectile interception queue. Pulse lasers use very short-range immediate delivery with high accuracy, greater close-range raw output than the particle beam and sixteen separate low-damage pulses per round. Armor absorption is applied to each pulse rather than an aggregated fleet total, allowing heavy plating to resist weak pulses. These are weapon roles rather than role-dependent bonuses to fighter or escort hulls.

Ion weapons are separate from particle beams. Ion shield drain removes recoverable charge; normal funded recharge restores it without repairing the emitter. All penetrating ion output damages electronics through shorts or overloads and none damages the hull. The application declares a 100% electronic damage share and controls its multiplier. Electronic damage selects powered hardware and electrical storage, respects each mount's finite durability, survives save/load and uses existing paid repair paths. It degrades and eventually disables sensors, weapons, shield emitters, propulsion controls, workshops, generators and batteries while leaving the hull intact. No automatic temporary shutdown timer is introduced.

All ranges, pulse counts, accuracy parameters, shield bypass, armor thresholds, armor absorption, electronic shares, projectile speeds and interception eligibility come from application data captured in blueprints. Old captured modules without new interaction fields retain neutral legacy behavior. Scalar projectile flight remains bounded to the tactical engagement, advances in 60-second rounds and is discarded at withdrawal or engagement end. Standalone wings and batteries use their captured weapons for pulse effects and delayed delivery and must carry finite tactical supplies before firing.

## Shared assessment and combat inputs

ShipCombatStats supplies deterministic installed firepower, active remaining shields and capped armor mitigation to both FleetThreatAssessment and TacticalCombatProcessor. Hull damage scales weapon output using the existing structural-score health convention, capped at full output. Destroyed ships cannot fire. Effective shield charge is bounded by installed capacity, so a positive legacy shield value without an emitter gives no protection.

Recorded essential-power failure disables weapon output and active shield protection. Modern electrical snapshots also need available battery charge, compatible generator fuel or deployed functioning solar arrays. Continuous combat-module demand is condition-scaled equipment idle load behind critical, cargo and scheduled drive requirements. Recorded weapon shedding disables firing and shield shedding disables active protection without destroying stored charge. New funded tactical accounting reevaluates these services. Legacy designs without an electrical profile use installed hardware and any recorded essential-power failure; this compatibility path does not grant equipment.

The fleet estimate remains the square root of combined available firepower times effective remaining endurance. FleetThreatAssessment quotes the next 60-second salvo through ShipCombatPower.fundedFirepower using current generator fuel, battery energy, output and discharge limits. Essential and cargo upkeep take priority exactly as in combat. Only the funded share of installed output contributes strength. Fight-or-escape assessment includes only mounts that can reach a currently detected opposing fleet at actual separation. The same tactical stance modifiers apply and random firing variance remains excluded. Endurance uses current hull and active shield charge with the same capped armor deflection used by damage resolution; future recharge or repairs do not count as existing defense. An unarmed or electrically unfunded force has no offensive strength and cannot authorize automatic defensive interception merely from its role.

FleetThreatAssessment.engagementStrength counts each fleet's salvo once at its nearest opposing fleet, then combines available firepower and current endurance across the group. An outranged fleet can therefore choose escape even when its total installed potential is larger. If neither side can reach the other, current strength is zero and the live contact produces HOLD rather than a range-based escape. The provisional overwhelming ratio remains greater than two. Hostile reach is a conservative hardware threat quote; it does not claim that the enemy currently has targeting contact or authorize either side to fire.

FleetThreatAssessment.strength retains full funded potential for approach planning and stored observations. Defensive interception compares its own potential against the detected enemy group's potential before committing a closing leg, so an out-of-range enemy does not become an artificially weak approach target. Safety-only caution retains potential comparisons when recent lost observations exist because their current range is unknown, while preserving any live range-based ESCAPE decision. It never reads hidden current equipment or trajectories. These quotes consume no resources and add no save fields; version remains 62.

The quote creates immutable projected power states and discards them. Repeated assessment consumes no fuel or battery energy, grants no shield charge and changes no ship or order. Optional shield recharge is omitted because it follows firing and cannot increase that salvo's funded output. The estimate covers the next round rather than guaranteeing sustained combat endurance. Both friendly and currently detected hostile fleets use the same quote. Internal simulation access to hostile equipment and stores does not share blueprints, research or additional UI intelligence with the observing empire. Legacy designs retain the existing electrical compatibility path with unknown firing energy.

Campaign combat and explicit-input tactical engagements resolve paid application-defined hits at actual separation. Stance scales paid output while application accuracy determines hit or miss. The old round-number range brackets, random damage variance and hardcoded subsystem damage bonuses have been removed. Subsystem orders still select physical collateral targets. Shield visuals use installed capacity and active charge. Reattachment, fuel, cargo and physical movement are not changed by the combat calculation. Reading hostile installed designs inside the simulation does not give the observing empire access to foreign research or blueprint ownership.

## Combat withdrawal

The campaign encounter adapter reassesses both surviving fleets after each simultaneous 60-second round using the updated hull, mount damage, shields, fuel and battery stores. It uses current shared sensor contacts and nearby own support through FleetThreatAssessment. A threat exceeding twice local friendly strength invokes the existing coordinated physical escape planner before another round fires.

A successful funded departure stops the engagement. Unavailable motion, protected supply commitments, passenger shortages or a failed group departure quote leave combat running while either side retains live contact. A side without current targeting holds fire; losing both live views ends further rounds. There is no teleport, free fuel or guaranteed escape from every orbit. Shots authorized at the start of the completed round remain simultaneous. The provisional daily encounter still resolves discrete rounds without advancing orbital positions within each minute.

Combat withdrawal may suspend explicit pursuit while retaining the original order in saved recovery intent. Ordinary pre-combat protection processing keeps explicit pursuit precedence. Guard, escort and existing automatic recovery identity survive withdrawal. The usual tick-owned return path waits for safety, checks compatible fleet fuel sharing and may split underfueled ships for reachable refuelling bases or nearby transport delivery before physical regrouping and mission restoration. Carrier-wing and planetary-battery entry points without campaign state retain their existing standalone resolution.

CombatWithdrawalTest checks post-damage reassessment, blocked fuel departure, coordinated nearby support, retained escort membership, explicit pursuit persistence, current sensor loss and paid guard return after peace.

## Live combat targeting

Campaign encounters require at least one side to detect the other through actual shared sensor hardware. Co-location, total war, stale detected-fleet IDs and last-known observations alone cannot start a battle. Before each 60-second round, each owner independently checks live coverage against the updated surviving fleets. The query includes currently authorized fleet and station observers, domestic corporations and direct diplomatic grants; it does not grant blueprints or relay foreign observations.

A fleet with no live target contact holds fire and spends no weapon salvo energy. It still pays required upkeep and policy-eligible shield recharge while the other side can continue shooting. Mount damage and sensor power shedding from a completed round affect the next round's authorization. Another authorized observer can maintain targeting and actual reacquisition permits firing in a following round. Simultaneous shots already authorized at the start of a round remain valid. If neither side retains live contact, further rounds stop without paying another combat interval. This is loss of targeting, not physical retreat or guaranteed safety.

Recent observations remain available to shield eligibility, defensive power and repair priorities and safe return gates but never authorize combat fire. Existing funded physical withdrawal remains based on live threats and does not gain hidden trajectories. Context-free tactical math and the standalone arena demo retain their existing explicit-input resolution; the authoritative campaign adapter supplies the live targeting predicate. ## Weapon-specific point defense

Point defense is an application of an existing weapon type. Developing each defensive variant requires completed development of its matching weapon: laser point defense follows laser weapons, a kinetic variant must follow its matching kinetic weapon and an ion variant must follow ion weapon development. Broad defensive research or another weapon type cannot substitute. Each variant can have its own range, power, ammunition and interception limits. The first kinetic variant uses mass drivers as described below. Ion uses the existing particle-beam development as described below. Other kinetic variants remain future mechanics.

### Ion point defense

The `pd_ion_beams` application belongs to electromagnetic weapons and requires completed `ion_weapons` development. Particle beams are a separate physical-damage application. Construction of `mod_ship_ion_point_defense` requires owned electricity, energy fields, electromagnetic weapons, ion weapons and that defensive application. Laser, mass-driver or generic defensive research cannot substitute. Foreign development grants no construction rights and research never fits existing ships.

Ion shares the typed defensive planner and paid combat path with laser and kinetic mounts. Whole funded defensive shots precede offense and optional shield recharge. Mixed mounts assign each missile once in blueprint order and cheaper eligible mounts can use power insufficient for an ion shot. Hull and mount damage reduce whole capacity; disabled mounts cannot intercept. Ships may defend while evading if incoming detection and required power are available. Ion interception removes the missile before ordinary impact; overflow still uses the existing damage path. This first implementation does not simulate gradual electronic disruption, beam travel or ion feedstock consumption and grants no offensive ship damage.

## Funded weapon firing

Combat equipment allocation keeps sensors first, then weapons and shields before workshops. Funded salvo energy can displace workshop idle draw while preserving critical and cargo upkeep. Evasive rounds put shields first and can shed weapon and workshop idle demand to fund recharge after sensor demand is considered. Both strength quotes and actual tactical rounds use these activity rules. Shield charging requires funded emitters for the whole round and retains the required arrival reserve. See [ShipRepairs.md](ShipRepairs.md#activity-aware-power-allocation).

Each provisional 60-second tactical round requests one salvo from each working installed weapon mount. CombatModuleCatalog stores weaponSalvoKwh alongside its damage. Cannons provisionally cost 1 kWh, lasers 8 kWh and mass drivers 12 kWh per healthy mount's salvo, in addition to their continuous maintenance demand. Mount condition and the existing hull-output factor scale energy-only salvo demand and damage. Ammunition shots retain their full per-shot energy and round consumption while damage scales with condition. Disabled or destroyed mounts request no firing energy. Modern profiles must capture installed weapon maintenance before they can fire. Research unlocks the hardware rather than granting free ammunition or another empire's blueprint.

ShipCombatPower uses one electrical interval for the whole minute. Essential and passenger services receive power first, cargo preservation next, weapon salvos next and optional shield recharge last. Carried compatible generator fuel and batteries provide the power; combat assumes no sunlight. Generator output, fuel exhaustion, battery charge and discharge limits all apply. Generator ingredients are actually consumed and weapon shortages do not invent a propulsion outage. If required upkeep cannot be funded for the round, weapons remain idle. A bounded quote reduces firing when a larger salvo would exhaust finite stores before the minute's remaining upkeep is supplied. The delivered share scales energy-only salvo damage proportionally. Ammunition weapons fire only whole shots with complete shot energy; rejected electrical quotes consume nothing.

Shield recharge can use remaining output only after the requested salvo is funded and post-firing stores cover the existing 48-hour essential and cargo reserve. The optional recharge quote recomputes the same minute from immutable opening stores; it does not grant another minute of generation or pay upkeep twice. Combat firing itself can spend carried arrival reserves to survive an engagement. Ordinary return and recovery checks must subsequently validate the stores that remain. Evading ships request no salvo and can use otherwise available power for recharge. Full shields stop additional charging.

Both sides fund their output before damage is applied, preserving simultaneous return fire. Stance affects paid output and each application supplies its accuracy and hit effects without charging extra energy for a miss. Logs show requested and delivered salvo kWh for each firing ship. Paid output remains valid even when a fully funded shot consumes the last stored energy; later rounds cannot reuse that energy. Electrical stores and shield charge already persist in save version 59, so this mechanic adds no saved field.

Legacy designs without electrical profiles retain their existing installed-hardware compatibility path and have unknown firing energy. This does not represent funded modern combat. Carrier wings and planetary batteries retain separate existing paths; their own energy and ammunition accounting await their respective mechanics. Coefficients establish the mechanism rather than balance targets.

## Powered shield recharge

Surviving installed emitters can recharge while stationary, travelling or fighting. Each healthy emitter provisionally supports 200 charge points and up to 10 points per hour. Charging consumes two kWh per delivered point in addition to the emitter's existing 30 kW continuous maintenance demand. Mount condition scales both capacity and recharge rate. Missing, disabled or destroyed hardware grants no recharge. Modern designs must have an electrical profile that includes the emitter maintenance demand; legacy compatibility alone cannot create charge.

Essential ship loads, passenger upkeep, cargo preservation and propulsion receive power before shield charging. The same solar, generator fuel recipes, output limits, battery discharge limits and eclipse intervals account for the optional load. Only actually delivered energy becomes shield charge. Partial supply can yield partial recharge without reporting an invented essential or drive failure. Full shields stop the extra load and charge never exceeds surviving installed capacity.

An immutable baseline first accounts for required loads without recharge. Recharge is admitted only when those loads remain supplied and projected stores fund the remaining journey plus the existing 48-hour essential and cargo arrival reserve. A full optional load is tried first; a bounded 12-step search reduces it when stored energy limits work. Rejected quotes consume nothing. The selected accounting result commits actual electrical stores and charge together. Burn, coast and eclipse phases use the same accounting in travel previews and execution. Orbital service intervals protect future atomic auxiliary burns and scheduled supply accounting protects the remaining deliveries and braking. Optional quotes cover up to 64 daily intervals per forecast before required loads finish analytically; later real ticks reconsider charging from their current stores. An orbital departure with a queued interstellar crossing defers charging until the crossing so approach work cannot consume crossing electricity.

Travelling fleets save optional shield energy when their owner's shared sensor view has no currently detected hostile fleet in the same system. Existing charge is retained and emitter maintenance remains powered. A detected fleet belonging to an empire at total war enables recharge on the next power tick. Friendly scouts, domestic corporations and explicit sensor-sharing agreements contribute through the ordinary shared view; hidden enemies and exploration history do not enable charging. Stationary fleets recharge normally. This is a current-contact policy rather than a prediction that unseen travel is safe; future contacts can change the decision and forecasts are recalculated from actual stores. No invented baseline sensor is added to ships without installed sensor hardware.

Tactical combat enables recharge under automatic and always charge policies; conserve energy defers optional recharge in combat as well as travel and stationary operation. Each provisional combat round represents 60 elapsed seconds and pays essential and cargo upkeep, weapon salvos then optional recharge before applying simultaneous damage. Combat uses carried generator fuel and battery energy with no assumed sunlight. The rate is proportional to elapsed time and current emitter condition, so a healthy emitter can restore at most one sixth of a point per round. Shields do not instantly refill between shots. The daily recharge calculation precedes onboard and base repair, so newly restored emitter capacity receives no free charge on its repair tick. No automatic hangar service creates charge.

### Fleet shield policy
