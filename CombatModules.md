# Ship combat modules

## Implemented mechanics

Ship firepower comes from installed combat modules rather than dry mass or military role. CombatModuleCatalog defines provisional ship cannons, lasers, mass drivers, guided missile launchers and shield emitters. Multiple installed mounts add their damage or shield capacity. Unknown components and missing blueprints grant no invented weapons or shields.

| Hardware | Module ID | Required owned research | Provisional capability |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ship cannon | `mod_ship_gun` | Electricity and industrial production | 80 damage, 1 kWh per salvo and 100 km maximum range |
| Ship laser | `mod_ship_laser` | Electricity, energy fields, energy weapons and laser weapons | 160 damage, 8 kWh per salvo and 1,000 km maximum range |
| Ship mass driver | `mod_ship_mass_driver` | Electricity, energy fields, electromagnetic weapons and mass drivers | 220 damage, 12 kWh per salvo and 500 km maximum range |
| Guided missile launcher | `mod_ship_guided_missile_launcher` | Electricity, industrial production and rocketry | 180 delayed damage, 2 kWh and one 100 kg missile per launch, 2,000 km maximum range |
| Laser point defense | `mod_ship_laser_point_defense` | Electricity, energy fields, energy weapons, laser weapons and laser point-defense application | Up to two missile interceptions per minute, 1 kWh each and 50 km defensive range |
| Mass-driver point defense | `mod_ship_kinetic_point_defense` | Electricity, energy fields, electromagnetic weapons, mass drivers and mass-driver point-defense application | Up to three missile interceptions per minute, 0.5 kWh and one 5 kg cartridge each, 25 km defensive range |
| Shield emitter | `mod_ship_shield` | Electricity, energy fields and shields | 200 shield capacity |

These values establish mechanisms rather than balance targets. Technology unlocks hardware for new designs. Research does not add weapons to existing ships, change their saved module IDs or share another empire's blueprints. ShipBlueprintFactory checks the owner's research and includes module slots, mass, complexity and electrical demand in validation. ConstructionMaterialCatalog adds each combat module's physical material receipt to the ship bill. Normal construction consumes that bill and commissioning uses installed shield capacity instead of a fixed free shield allowance.

The ship designer offers researched hardware counts using the injected snapshot. Local previews include their physical and electrical costs. Saving continues through ordinary design commands and tick validation; the frontend does not mutate ships. Existing hardware remains visible while editing an unused design and in-use designs retain their existing edit restriction.

## Shared assessment and combat inputs

ShipCombatStats supplies deterministic installed firepower, active remaining shields and capped armor mitigation to both FleetThreatAssessment and TacticalCombatProcessor. Hull damage scales weapon output using the existing structural-score health convention, capped at full output. Destroyed ships cannot fire. Effective shield charge is bounded by installed capacity, so a positive legacy shield value without an emitter gives no protection.

Recorded essential-power failure disables weapon output and active shield protection. Modern electrical snapshots also need available battery charge, compatible generator fuel or deployed functioning solar arrays. Continuous combat-module demand is condition-scaled equipment idle load behind critical, cargo and scheduled drive requirements. Recorded weapon shedding disables firing and shield shedding disables active protection without destroying stored charge. New funded tactical accounting reevaluates these services. Legacy designs without an electrical profile use installed hardware and any recorded essential-power failure; this compatibility path does not grant equipment.

The fleet estimate remains the square root of combined available firepower times effective remaining endurance. FleetThreatAssessment quotes the next 60-second salvo through ShipCombatPower.fundedFirepower using current generator fuel, battery energy, output and discharge limits. Essential and cargo upkeep take priority exactly as in combat. Only the funded share of installed output contributes strength. Fight-or-escape assessment includes only mounts that can reach a currently detected opposing fleet at actual separation. The same tactical stance modifiers apply and random firing variance remains excluded. Endurance uses current hull and active shield charge with the same capped armor deflection used by damage resolution; future recharge or repairs do not count as existing defense. An unarmed or electrically unfunded force has no offensive strength and cannot authorize automatic defensive interception merely from its role.

FleetThreatAssessment.engagementStrength counts each fleet's salvo once at its nearest opposing fleet, then combines available firepower and current endurance across the group. An outranged fleet can therefore choose escape even when its total installed potential is larger. If neither side can reach the other, current strength is zero and the live contact produces HOLD rather than a range-based escape. The provisional overwhelming ratio remains greater than two. Hostile reach is a conservative hardware threat quote; it does not claim that the enemy currently has targeting contact or authorize either side to fire.

FleetThreatAssessment.strength retains full funded potential for approach planning and stored observations. Defensive interception compares its own potential against the detected enemy group's potential before committing a closing leg, so an out-of-range enemy does not become an artificially weak approach target. Safety-only caution retains potential comparisons when recent lost observations exist because their current range is unknown, while preserving any live range-based ESCAPE decision. It never reads hidden current equipment or trajectories. These quotes consume no resources and add no save fields; version remains 62.

The quote creates immutable projected power states and discards them. Repeated assessment consumes no fuel or battery energy, grants no shield charge and changes no ship or order. Optional shield recharge is omitted because it follows firing and cannot increase that salvo's funded output. The estimate covers the next round rather than guaranteeing sustained combat endurance. Both friendly and currently detected hostile fleets use the same quote. Internal simulation access to hostile equipment and stores does not share blueprints, research or additional UI intelligence with the observing empire. Legacy designs retain the existing electrical compatibility path with unknown firing energy.

Campaign combat uses actual separation and installed weapon ranges with provisional random firing variance and subsystem-target damage modifiers. Context-free tactical demos retain their existing range brackets, strike wings and planetary batteries. Ship firing and damage absorption read shared current stats each round. Shield visuals use installed capacity and active charge. Reattachment, fuel, cargo and physical movement are not changed by the combat calculation. Reading hostile installed designs inside the simulation does not give the observing empire access to foreign research or blueprint ownership.

## Combat withdrawal

The campaign encounter adapter reassesses both surviving fleets after each simultaneous 60-second round using the updated hull, mount damage, shields, fuel and battery stores. It uses current shared sensor contacts and nearby own support through FleetThreatAssessment. A threat exceeding twice local friendly strength invokes the existing coordinated physical escape planner before another round fires.

A successful funded departure stops the engagement. Unavailable motion, protected supply commitments, passenger shortages or a failed group departure quote leave combat running while either side retains live contact. A side without current targeting holds fire; losing both live views ends further rounds. There is no teleport, free fuel or guaranteed escape from every orbit. Shots authorized at the start of the completed round remain simultaneous. The provisional daily encounter still resolves discrete rounds without advancing orbital positions within each minute.

Combat withdrawal may suspend explicit pursuit while retaining the original order in saved recovery intent. Ordinary pre-combat protection processing keeps explicit pursuit precedence. Guard, escort and existing automatic recovery identity survive withdrawal. The usual tick-owned return path waits for safety, checks compatible fleet fuel sharing and may split underfueled ships for reachable refuelling bases or nearby transport delivery before physical regrouping and mission restoration. Carrier-wing and planetary-battery entry points without campaign state retain their existing standalone resolution.

CombatWithdrawalTest checks post-damage reassessment, blocked fuel departure, coordinated nearby support, retained escort membership, explicit pursuit persistence, current sensor loss and paid guard return after peace.

## Live combat targeting

Campaign encounters require at least one side to detect the other through actual shared sensor hardware. Co-location, total war, stale detected-fleet IDs and last-known observations alone cannot start a battle. Before each 60-second round, each owner independently checks live coverage against the updated surviving fleets. The query includes currently authorized fleet and station observers, domestic corporations and direct diplomatic grants; it does not grant blueprints or relay foreign observations.

A fleet with no live target contact holds fire and spends no weapon salvo energy. It still pays required upkeep and policy-eligible shield recharge while the other side can continue shooting. Mount damage and sensor power shedding from a completed round affect the next round's authorization. Another authorized observer can maintain targeting and actual reacquisition permits firing in a following round. Simultaneous shots already authorized at the start of a round remain valid. If neither side retains live contact, further rounds stop without paying another combat interval. This is loss of targeting, not physical retreat or guaranteed safety.

Recent observations remain available to shield eligibility, defensive power and repair priorities and safe return gates but never authorize combat fire. Existing funded physical withdrawal remains based on live threats and does not gain hidden trajectories. Context-free tactical math and the standalone arena demo retain their existing explicit-input resolution; the authoritative campaign adapter supplies the live targeting predicate. Save version remains 62.

CombatSensorContactTest covers no encounter from stale or remembered contacts, unilateral live fire, actual sensor power shedding, continued targeting through a shared observer, loss and reacquisition and stopping both-blind rounds without another salvo payment. TacticalFleetEncounterResolverTest explicitly installs sensors on separate owned designs.

## Weapon engagement ranges

Installed cannons, mass drivers and lasers have provisional inclusive maximum ranges of 100 km, 500 km and 1,000 km respectively. ShipWeaponRange computes actual separation using the shared system-centered sensor geometry, including moving vector and native orbital states. Identical stationary sites are co-located; missing geometry or a different system supplies no range authorization. Sensor detection can extend much farther and never substitutes for weapon reach.

Each surviving mount contributes damage and salvo energy only when the actual separation is within its own range. Partial mount condition scales direct weapon damage and energy but retains the maximum range until disabled. Missiles retain whole ammunition and full launch energy requirements while damage scales with condition. Mixed ships and fleets can therefore fire only their longer-range weapons without spending energy on shorter-range salvos. Ordinary continuous equipment upkeep, paid output limits and optional shield rules still apply. Campaign rounds use actual separation instead of the old round-number range multiplier; stance and provisional direct damage variance remain. Context-free tactical demos retain their legacy close-range brackets.

Campaign encounters can begin between distinct sites or moving fleets before velocity-matched rendezvous when at least one side has both live detection and a surviving weapon in range. Each round pays only eligible mount salvos. Relative velocity mismatch does not prevent firing, though it still prevents confirmed physical rendezvous. Already funded motion continues; combat does not advance orbital positions within its provisional minute rounds, redirect trajectories or invent braking. History uses WEAPON_RANGE for ranged encounters and retains RENDEZVOUS for confirmed moving contact.

Interceptors outside reach can select a funded stand-off parking orbit against a stationary target around the same body or a velocity-matched firing offset against a detected planetary or lunar coast when they have a range advantage. Candidate arrival geometry and funded firing are checked after paid travel and braking; see [InterceptOrders.md](InterceptOrders.md#funded-stand-off-parking) and [moving stand-off](InterceptOrders.md#moving-stand-off-around-a-body). Other approaches retain their existing paths. An idle interceptor already within reach does not launch an unnecessary new leg. Guards and escorts likewise hold for an in-range engagement. A committed approach can encounter combat before its endpoint but continues its physical itinerary. Stationary intercept completion recognizes weapon reach after the encounter phase. Continuous pursuit steering, line of sight, flight-time ballistics and range-dependent accuracy remain future mechanics. Ship designer tooltips show maximum range. No save field is added and version remains 62.

ShipWeaponRangeTest checks inclusive boundaries, excluded salvo payment, mixed and damaged mounts, actual moving separation, detection beyond weapon range, asymmetric long-range fire and preserved motion. InterceptFleetCommandTest checks holding position within reach without spending approach resources. BodyFleetRendezvousTest retains physical matching checks from an out-of-range launch fixture and distinguishes velocity mismatch and one-sided detection from firing permission.

## Missile weapons

The first guided launcher requires owned electricity, industrial production and rocketry research. Its provisional module occupies two medium slots, weighs 1,000 kg and draws 10 kW continuously. Construction adds 100 kg steel and 50 kg silicon. Installed damage and paid repairs use ordinary mount rules. Research never fits or upgrades existing hulls and other empires' research or blueprints grant no construction rights.

Each working in-range launcher can launch one whole packaged `guided_missile` per 60-second round. Each cartridge occupies 100 kg of ordinary cargo including its motor, guidance and warhead. Launchers start empty. Whole carried rounds limit duplicate mounts in blueprint order; residual mass below 100 kg remains cargo but cannot launch. Required upkeep, funded point defense and funded direct salvos precede missile launches. A launch consumes exactly 2 kWh and one cartridge; insufficient output never pays a fractional launch or consumes fractional ammunition. Damage scales with current hull and mount condition and the launching stance, without reducing cartridge mass or launch energy. Quotes project immutable stores and discard the result. Legacy ships without an electrical profile cannot launch missiles.

Provisional maximum reach is 2,000 km and flight speed is 10 km/s. A paid launch enters a flight-time queue after its opening round. Subsequent rounds advance it by 60 seconds; it cannot damage the enemy during the launch round. Flight takes at least one later round and otherwise uses launch separation divided by speed, rounded up to a combat interval. Impact enters the existing simultaneous fleet damage, shield, armor and mount-damage path. Missile launch actions report zero damage; separate impact actions report actual incoming damage. Threat and stand-off quotes count funded launch potential rather than immediate hits. Empty magazines remove missile reach and offensive potential.

Guidance requires the launching side's live shared sensor contact at every following round opening, using the same authorized empire and corporate observer rules as ordinary fire. Loss of contact permanently self-destructs that side's pending missiles; later reacquisition cannot resurrect them. No memory or hidden target lookup provides guidance. Withdrawal, destruction of either fleet or the ten-round engagement boundary ends pending flights with an explicit self-destruct log and no refund. The complete tactical engagement runs atomically within one campaign tick, so no flight crosses save boundaries. Remaining ammunition, installed launchers, damage and paid electrical stores persist through existing save version 62 fields.

The `guided_missile_mfg` rocketry application uses the existing paid powered industrial path. Its provisional recipe uses 60 kg steel, 10 kg silicon, 20 kg hydrocarbons and 10 kg nitrates per 100 kg product, with 10 workers per batch and 500 kW draw. It requires owned electricity, rocketry and industrial production. Normal recipe demand creates a hub order; hub cash, storage, inputs, manufacturing capacity, staffing and power still constrain production and sales. Existing proactive stock targets can maintain ammunition buffers. Buy and load ammunition through ship cargo supplies. Orbital loading admits launcher-equipped ships for this ammunition and retains local stock, purchase, cargo capacity and paid lift checks. Surface loading uses the existing surface command. There is no automatic free reload.

Fleet cards show whole carried rounds and the designer includes launcher counts, normal idle demand and full combat launch power. Tactical torpedo visuals and carrier-wing reports remain separate provisional representations. This first model uses scalar flight time and the existing frozen tactical separation, rather than moving projectile trajectories or terminal maneuver simulation. Laser point defense intercepts approaching missiles as described below. Autonomous seekers and missiles continuing across disengagement remain future mechanics.

## Weapon-specific point defense

Point defense is an application of an existing weapon type. Developing each defensive variant requires completed development of its matching weapon: laser point defense follows laser weapons, a kinetic variant must follow its matching kinetic weapon and an ion variant must follow ion weapon development. Broad defensive research or another weapon type cannot substitute. Each variant can have its own range, power, ammunition and interception limits. The first kinetic variant uses mass drivers as described below. Ion and other kinetic variants remain future mechanics.

The implemented `pd_lasers` application belongs to energy weapons and requires completed `laser_weapons` development before research can begin. The `mod_ship_laser_point_defense` module requires owned electricity, energy fields, energy weapons, laser weapons and `pd_lasers` before construction. The legacy `defensive_weapons` catalog entry grants no installed laser point-defense capability and is not a prerequisite for this application. Research does not fit existing ships or share blueprints between empires.

A provisional healthy laser point-defense mount occupies one small slot, weighs 500 kg, consumes 50 kg industrial ceramics and 25 kg silicon in construction and draws 20 kW continuously. It contributes no offensive damage or offensive weapon range. Its 50 km defensive reach permits up to two whole missile interceptions per 60-second round at 1 kWh each. Hull and mount condition scale capacity before rounding down per mount; duplicate working mounts add their capacities. Disabled hardware cannot intercept. General collateral, targeted weapon damage and paid weapon repairs include defensive mounts.

Existing flight timers estimate each already-launched missile's distance at the end of the next interval. A missile entering defensive range during that interval can be intercepted before timer advancement applies its impact. Missiles outside that reach consume no active defense power. Detection comes from current authorized shared contact with the launching fleet or working sensors in the defending fleet covering the approaching missile's modeled distance. This allows local incoming detection without seeing the launcher. Historical contacts grant no defense targeting. The scalar model does not yet provide separate campaign sensor contacts or three-dimensional trajectories for missiles.

ShipCombatPower pays essential and cargo upkeep, then whole defensive shots, direct salvos, whole missile launches and optional shield recharge. Continuous service loads retain their existing activity rules. Active defense requests combat power priority even while evading; the fleet's offensive stance does not disable defensive shots. Generator output, discharge limits, carried fuel and battery energy constrain paid work. Insufficient power never destroys a missile with a fractional shot or spends fractional defense energy. Ships in a fleet pay in existing list order and each removed missile is unavailable to later defenders, preventing duplicate charges. Missiles whose launching side has already lost guidance self-destruct without paid interception.

Paid interception removes the missile before the ordinary impact path and reports a `POINT_DEFENSE` action with zero ship damage. Surviving missiles retain their paid launch damage and apply normal shields, armor and hull damage. No stance automatically supplies interception and legacy ships without an electrical profile get no free defense. Threat assessment continues to count funded offensive output separately; defensive capacity is not converted into invented offensive damage.

The designer exposes the researched module and includes its idle demand and maximum active defensive shots in full combat power. Existing module IDs, damage and paid electrical stores persist at save version 62. Whole interceptions use provisional deterministic capacity; automatic rebalancing, probabilistic accuracy, carrier interception and ion defensive weapons remain future mechanics.

### Kinetic point defense

The first kinetic variant is mass-driver point defense. The `pd_mass_drivers` application belongs to electromagnetic weapons and requires completed `mass_drivers` development. Construction of `mod_ship_kinetic_point_defense` also requires owned electricity, energy fields, electromagnetic weapons and that defensive application. Laser development or a generic defensive technology cannot substitute for the matching mass driver. Future ion or other kinetic defensive variants must likewise require their matching weapon development.

Provisional hardware occupies one small slot, weighs 500 kg and draws 15 kW continuously. Its construction receipt adds 100 kg steel and 25 kg tungsten ore. A healthy mount can intercept up to three missiles per minute within 25 km. Each successful whole interception consumes 0.5 kWh and one packaged `kinetic_pd_round` burst cartridge weighing 5 kg. Cartridges occupy ordinary cargo space. Empty magazines cannot intercept; residual mass below one cartridge remains aboard. Duplicate mounts share finite carried ammunition. Hull and mount condition reduce whole capacity using the same defensive damage and repair rules as lasers.

Both types use one planner. Observed approaching missiles are ordered by closest modeled distance and assigned once in blueprint mount order. Each mount retains its own range, shot energy and ammunition requirement. Paid work is replanned against actual available energy, so an unaffordable laser shot can leave its target to a funded kinetic mount within kinetic reach. Only whole funded shots consume cartridges and energy. Ship-list order still prevents duplicate payments across defenders. Surviving missiles apply the normal impact path, including missiles exceeding defensive capacity or arriving after ammunition runs out. This bounded assignment does not optimize target matching or rebalance combat coefficients.

The `kinetic_pd_ammunition_mfg` electromagnetic-weapon application uses existing paid industry. Its recipe requires owned electricity, energy fields, electromagnetic weapons, mass drivers, mass-driver point defense and industrial production. Each provisional batch consumes 4 kg steel and 1 kg tungsten ore to produce 5 kg packaged ammunition, with one worker per batch and 100 kW facility draw. Existing manufacturing capacity, staffing, power, stock, funds and hub storage gates apply. Recipe demand creates the ordinary hub order and configured proactive stock targets can replenish it. No ship receives free ammunition at construction or reload.

Existing cargo supplies buy and load cartridges; orbital loading admits kinetic-defense-equipped combat ships specifically for this ammunition and preserves paid lift, local purchase and capacity checks. Fleet cards show whole kinetic cartridges. The designer includes both defensive types' continuous demand and maximum active shot energy in full combat power. Saved cargo, module IDs, damage and electrical stores preserve depletion at save version 62 without new fields. Direct mass-driver ammunition and three-dimensional kinetic interception remain separate future mechanics.

## Persistent mount damage


Sensor arrays also receive general hull-penetrating collateral and use the same saved mount condition and paid repair paths. Their remaining condition scales live detection range and survey strength without changing frozen blueprint capability. Current shared coverage still includes any other authorized working observers. See [SensorModules.md](SensorModules.md#detection-and-integration).

Each ship stores immutable ShipModuleDamage keyed by the module's position in its blueprint list. Repeated module IDs represent separate mounts. In-use designs cannot be edited, keeping mount identity stable. Missing entries mean healthy hardware; damage ranges from zero to a provisional 100 points per mount. Condition linearly scales that mount's weapon output or shield capacity and a disabled mount contributes zero. Hull condition and electrical readiness still apply to the resulting weapon output. Damage never changes the blueprint or another ship using it.

Only damage that penetrates active shields and armor into the hull can damage hardware. General hits allocate 25% of the penetrating damage equally across surviving installed weapons, emitters and repair workshops. Targeting weapons or shields instead allocates the penetrating damage to that hardware type in blueprint order, overflowing from disabled mounts to the next eligible mount. These provisional coefficients verify the mechanism rather than define balance. Existing targeting modifiers remain; engine and warp targeting still affect hull damage without persistent drive damage. A shield hit absorbed entirely by shield charge cannot damage an emitter. After damage, charge is capped by the surviving emitter capacity.

Combat rereads current mount condition each round. FleetThreatAssessment uses the same condition through ShipCombatStats, so surviving damaged fleets lose estimated strength as well as actual capability. Fuel purchases, cargo handling, passenger changes, ownership changes, fleet membership, ordinary power ticks and physical travel preserve the state. Equipped base workshops now provide paid hull and module repair; see [BaseRepairs.md](BaseRepairs.md). No automatic equipment replacement or free restoration is introduced.

## Funded weapon firing

Combat equipment allocation keeps sensors first, then weapons and shields before workshops. Funded salvo energy can displace workshop idle draw while preserving critical and cargo upkeep. Evasive rounds put shields first and can shed weapon and workshop idle demand to fund recharge after sensor demand is considered. Both strength quotes and actual tactical rounds use these activity rules. Shield charging requires funded emitters for the whole round and retains the required arrival reserve. See [ShipRepairs.md](ShipRepairs.md#activity-aware-power-allocation).

Each provisional 60-second tactical round requests one salvo from each working installed weapon mount. CombatModuleCatalog stores weaponSalvoKwh alongside its damage. Cannons provisionally cost 1 kWh, lasers 8 kWh and mass drivers 12 kWh per healthy mount's salvo, in addition to their continuous maintenance demand. Mount condition and the existing hull-output factor scale both requested energy and damage. Disabled or destroyed mounts request no firing energy. Modern profiles must capture installed weapon maintenance before they can fire. Research unlocks the hardware rather than granting free ammunition or another empire's blueprint.

ShipCombatPower uses one electrical interval for the whole minute. Essential and passenger services receive power first, cargo preservation next, weapon salvos next and optional shield recharge last. Carried compatible generator fuel and batteries provide the power; combat assumes no sunlight. Generator output, fuel exhaustion, battery charge and discharge limits all apply. Generator ingredients are actually consumed and weapon shortages do not invent a propulsion outage. If required upkeep cannot be funded for the round, weapons remain idle. A bounded quote reduces firing when a larger salvo would exhaust finite stores before the minute's remaining upkeep is supplied. Otherwise the delivered share of requested salvo energy scales damage proportionally across the ship's working mounts. This represents a reduced salvo rather than discrete ammunition or an instantaneous capacitor discharge.

Shield recharge can use remaining output only after the requested salvo is funded and post-firing stores cover the existing 48-hour essential and cargo reserve. The optional recharge quote recomputes the same minute from immutable opening stores; it does not grant another minute of generation or pay upkeep twice. Combat firing itself can spend carried arrival reserves to survive an engagement. Ordinary return and recovery checks must subsequently validate the stores that remain. Evading ships request no salvo and can use otherwise available power for recharge. Full shields stop additional charging.

Both sides fund their output before damage is applied, preserving simultaneous return fire. Range brackets, stance and random variance affect the paid output without billing accuracy or stance modifiers as extra energy. Logs show requested and delivered salvo kWh for each firing ship. Paid output remains valid even when a fully funded shot consumes the last stored energy; later rounds cannot reuse that energy. Electrical stores and shield charge already persist in save version 59, so this mechanic adds no saved field.

Legacy designs without electrical profiles retain their existing installed-hardware compatibility path and have unknown firing energy. This does not represent funded modern combat. Carrier wings and planetary batteries retain separate existing paths; their own energy and ammunition accounting await their respective mechanics. Coefficients establish the mechanism rather than balance targets.

## Designer power requirements

The ship designer displays normal-operation power in kW and the corresponding kWh per day. Normal demand includes captured continuous maintenance and the rated preservation load for full cargo capacity. The preview assumes healthy hardware and no passengers, so actual passenger or stasis upkeep is additional. Powered travel adds the drive's electrical demand separately.

Full combat adds the installed healthy salvo energy divided by the current tactical round duration and the maximum emitter recharge load. Both kW and kWh per 60-second round are shown, with salvo and recharge costs broken out. A separate combined figure adds propulsion to full combat demand; the current tactical processor accounts for firing and recharge while movement processors fund propulsion. Full shields stop additional recharge and damage or limited energy can lower actual usage. The requirement represents full requested output rather than a promise that the chosen power plant will supply it.

Rated fueled generation, solar output at Earth flux and maximum battery discharge are shown for comparison. Actual solar geometry, compatible fuel inventory, stored battery energy and service priorities still govern execution. The local cosmetic preview uses optimized selected module stats and the electrical profile without consuming resources, staging commands or changing blueprint validation. It updates when hardware changes and remains visible for a draft with failed physics validation.

## Powered shield recharge

Surviving installed emitters can recharge while stationary, travelling or fighting. Each healthy emitter provisionally supports 200 charge points and up to 10 points per hour. Charging consumes two kWh per delivered point in addition to the emitter's existing 30 kW continuous maintenance demand. Mount condition scales both capacity and recharge rate. Missing, disabled or destroyed hardware grants no recharge. Modern designs must have an electrical profile that includes the emitter maintenance demand; legacy compatibility alone cannot create charge.

Essential ship loads, passenger upkeep, cargo preservation and propulsion receive power before shield charging. The same solar, generator fuel recipes, output limits, battery discharge limits and eclipse intervals account for the optional load. Only actually delivered energy becomes shield charge. Partial supply can yield partial recharge without reporting an invented essential or drive failure. Full shields stop the extra load and charge never exceeds surviving installed capacity.

An immutable baseline first accounts for required loads without recharge. Recharge is admitted only when those loads remain supplied and projected stores fund the remaining journey plus the existing 48-hour essential and cargo arrival reserve. A full optional load is tried first; a bounded 12-step search reduces it when stored energy limits work. Rejected quotes consume nothing. The selected accounting result commits actual electrical stores and charge together. Burn, coast and eclipse phases use the same accounting in travel previews and execution. Orbital service intervals protect future atomic auxiliary burns and scheduled supply accounting protects the remaining deliveries and braking. Optional quotes cover up to 64 daily intervals per forecast before required loads finish analytically; later real ticks reconsider charging from their current stores. An orbital departure with a queued interstellar crossing defers charging until the crossing so approach work cannot consume crossing electricity.

Travelling fleets save optional shield energy when their owner's shared sensor view has no currently detected hostile fleet in the same system. Existing charge is retained and emitter maintenance remains powered. A detected fleet belonging to an empire at total war enables recharge on the next power tick. Friendly scouts, domestic corporations and explicit sensor-sharing agreements contribute through the ordinary shared view; hidden enemies and exploration history do not enable charging. Stationary fleets recharge normally. This is a current-contact policy rather than a prediction that unseen travel is safe; future contacts can change the decision and forecasts are recalculated from actual stores. No invented baseline sensor is added to ships without installed sensor hardware.

Tactical combat enables recharge under automatic and always charge policies; conserve energy defers optional recharge in combat as well as travel and stationary operation. Each provisional combat round represents 60 elapsed seconds and pays essential and cargo upkeep, weapon salvos then optional recharge before applying simultaneous damage. Combat uses carried generator fuel and battery energy with no assumed sunlight. The rate is proportional to elapsed time and current emitter condition, so a healthy emitter can restore at most one sixth of a point per round. Shields do not instantly refill between shots. The daily recharge calculation precedes onboard and base repair, so newly restored emitter capacity receives no free charge on its repair tick. No automatic hangar service creates charge.

### Fleet shield policy

Each fleet saves one recharge policy. Automatic retains stationary recharge, combat recharge and threat-aware travel eligibility, including recent last-known threats. Always charge requests optional recharge even during quiet travel. Conserve energy defers optional recharge everywhere while retaining existing charge and ordinary emitter idle upkeep. None of these policies changes equipment priority, creates energy, overrides lost hardware or spends protected travel and arrival reserves. Full shields stop additional charging.

Fleet cards offer a policy selector and queued apply action plus a snapshot-backed eligibility reason. The reason distinguishes stationary operation, live threats, recent observations, quiet travel and explicit policy. Eligibility is not a guarantee of funded charging; available power, working emitter condition and reserves still decide actual work. The control validates ownership and changes intent on the next simulation tick without altering stores or the current itinerary.

Save version 62 adds the immutable fleet policy with automatic as the older-save default. Ordinary copies, travel, interruption, recovery, forecasts and splits preserve it. Ships transferred into another fleet use the receiving fleet's policy. Joined escorts follow their convoy's policy as part of that fleet.

ShipCombatStats immediately uses the resulting charged protection for shared threat assessment and tactical combat. Shield health and electrical stores already persist in save version 59; this mechanic adds no saved field or version. Save/load continues partial recharge from actual remaining stores. Coefficients remain provisional mechanism values.

## Current limits

Ship repair workshops now join weapons and emitters in the general-hit damage pool. Workshop condition scales daily repair capacity and disablement stops work. Weapon and shield targeting does not select workshops. Researched powered workshops restore hull and mount condition using host materials, including work on other ships in the same owned fleet. See [ShipRepairs.md](ShipRepairs.md).

Module replacement and explicit module-condition UI await separate mechanics. Direct weapon ammunition, heat, penetration and dedicated firing capacitors also await separate mechanics. Missile ammunition and the one-launch-per-round limit are implemented as described above. Tactical demo range labels and projectile visuals remain provisional. Carrier-wing and planetary-battery inputs retain their separate existing tactical paths and are not included in the normal fleet threat estimate. Unknown enemy identification is not modeled by this change.

Save version 57 adds per-ship mount damage alongside the existing module IDs, shield charge and electrical state. Saves without module damage load healthy installed hardware. Older unarmed designs receive no automatic equipment. Their old nominal shield values are ignored by combat unless compatible emitters are installed.
