### Ship customization and the modular hull design interface

The ship design interface is a hard physics-bounded engineering playground. Instead of selecting from fixed, pre-defined hull size classes (such as "Destroyer" or "Cruiser"), users construct starframes completely from scratch. The interface dynamically calculates the ship's dry mass, slot limits, operational classification and launch restrictions based entirely on the user's custom choice of modules and structural materials.

#### Structural blueprint properties

Current implementation: The player workbench uses a fixed medium frame with 30 slots and 15,000 kg frame mass. `ShipComponentCatalog` supplies provisional reactor, cargo and stasis stats alongside the propulsion catalog. Registration and editing commands accept immutable material and component choices. `ShipBlueprintFactory` recalculates mass, cargo, fuel, power, structural integrity, thrust and manufacturing metadata using live research and yard capacity. Submitted calculated values cannot override these checks. Unknown components, unknown structural materials, invalid armor thickness, missing tanks, multiple drives, slot overflow, power deficits and unsupported role requirements are rejected.

Launch previews use the first non-gas planet with positive gravity in an owned system. Its atmosphere is provisionally treated as either vacuum or pressure 1; without a surface reference the blueprint is orbital only. Selecting a launch body and full atmospheric modeling remain future work. Existing saved designs retain their captured values. A blueprint referenced by a built ship or active construction order cannot be edited; register a new blueprint for a revised design. Corporate procurement retains its separate provisional hull template. Generator fuel consumption and full material-dependent armor physics remain future work.

Every user-defined ship configuration requires the allocation of a primary operational anchor role and a core structural material, which dictate the baseline scaling limits of the starframe:

- **Primary operational role:** The user tags the design with an explicit intent from the [User-Defined Ship Roles Matrix](Spaceships.md). This tag functions as a validation check (e.g., a *Troop Transport* blueprint must contain a *Cryogenic Troop Transport Bay*). It also dictates how automated private corporate networks or military AI fleets utilize the hull after manufacturing.
- **Starframe structural material:** The user selects a material from the [Materials Database](engine/src/main/resources/materials.json) to forge the ship's chassis skeleton and base outer plating. The choice of material alters three absolute properties per slot used:
    - `baseHullWeightMultiplier`: Affects the total dry mass.
    - `structuralStrengthCoefficient`: Dictates hull damage thresholds against kinetic and thermal weapons.
    - `manufacturingComplexityRating`: Restricts which orbital shipyards can assemble the blueprint.

#### The dynamic sizing and slot allocation engine

Hulls expand or contract dynamically based on the total sum of their equipped **Module Size Slots**. There are no artificial size limits; the scale of the vessel is an emergent output of its functional components:

- **Light starframes (1 to 15 total slots allocated):** Classified automatically as light craft (e.g., short-range fighters, bombers, light scouts or fast couriers). They possess high tactical evasion bonuses and minor dry mass footprints, making them incredibly cheap to launch past planetary gravity wells.
- **Medium starframes (16 to 50 total slots allocated):** Classified as medium hulls (e.g., escorts, system-defense gunships, explorers or specialized corporate cargo haulers). They strike a balance between structural durability and propulsion efficiency.
- **Heavy starframes (51 to 150+ total slots allocated):** Classified as heavy capital starframes (e.g., combat warships, carriers, deep-space construction trains, long-range logistics freighters or colony arks). They hold an immense physical footprint, making them highly optimal to manufacture exclusively within zero-G *Capital Mega-Engineering Slipways* to completely avoid planetary launch gravity penalties.

#### Slot classes and component formatting

Instead of using uniform, arbitrary item grids, a custom starframe layout handles modules using explicit **Slot Size Classes**. Modules from the [Spaceship Component Manifest](Spaceships.md) or utility manifests carry static slot size footprints that map directly onto the hull frame:

- **Small (S) slots [1 size slot]:** Optimized for compact, highly reactive utility and light tactical sub-systems. Examples include *Point-Defense Laser Grids*, maneuvering *RCS Thruster Arrays*, light *Biometric Passenger Cabin Arrays* and low-yield localized scanning arrays.
- **Medium (M) slots [2–6 size slots]:** Designed for standard ship operations and baseline frontline combat modules. Examples include *Continuous Beam Laser Mounts* (2 slots), *High-Impulse MPD Thrusters* (4 slots), *Baseline Fission Propulsion Drives* (4 slots).
- **Large (L) slots [8–30+ size slots]:** Reserved for hyper-dense, super-heavy macro-structures and capital-grade fleet components. Examples include *Advanced Fusion Propulsion Drives* (8 slots), *Bulk Cargo Vaults* (12 slots), *Spacetime Deformation Warp Drives* (16 slots) and *Planetary Colonization Modules* (30 slots).

#### The structural integrity ceiling ($SI_c$)

Hulls expand dynamically as slots are added, but a ship frame cannot be expanded infinitely without collapsing under its own structural stress or propulsion vectors. Every design must satisfy the **Structural Integrity Ceiling**:

Draft: $$SI_c = \frac{\text{Hull Frame Material Strength Coefficient}}{\text{Total Allocated Module Slots}} \times \left(1.0 - \text{Total Dry Mass Modifier}\right)$$

- **The material choice barrier:** If a user designs a massive starframe utilizing cheap `steel` or lightweight `refined_aluminum` to save on upfront costs, the low structural strength coefficient will flag a structural integrity failure if they attempt to squeeze more than 40 slots into the frame.
- **The nanotech pivot:** To successfully build a heavy capital starframe holding 100+ module slots (like a *Carrier Ship* or a multi-vault *Cargo Transport*), the design must utilize advanced composite materials like `carbon_nanotubes` or `silicon_carbide`. This ensures the frame can withstand the immense torsional forces generated by high-impulse propulsion drives without tearing itself apart.

#### Autonomous corporate ship design

Private corporations may contract shipyards to manufacture eligible public blueprints or design proprietary blueprints for their own use. A proprietary blueprint is owned by the designing corporation:
- **The shortcoming trigger:** If an empire's public design database lacks a ship specialized to solve a critical regional supply bottleneck, the corporate AI will independently initialize its own ship design cycle. For example, if a colony has a massive surplus of volatile ices but a public cargo design has a dry mass too heavy to escape the planet's gravity well profitably, a trade corporation will autonomously design a custom, hyper-lightweight *Cargo Transport* utilizing aluminum framing and minimal modules to clear the deficit.
- **Proprietary blueprint access:** Only the corporation that owns a proprietary blueprint may commission ships from it. The player must not be offered that blueprint in a normal ship construction flow. Purchasing a finished ship from a corporate shipyard transfers the ship to the player but does not transfer its blueprint or manufacturing rights.
- **Yard fee revenues:** When a private corporation designs a proprietary hull and pays universal credits to manufacture it at a state-owned *Orbital Shipyard Assembly Grid*, the state public treasury directly skims revenue from the contract, turning autonomous corporate design initiatives into a massive financial booster for the empire.

#### The external armor interface

To maintain total structural clarity within the interface, **Hull Armor Plating is completely separated from the slot formatting engine**.
- **No slot allocation:** A user or corporation never spends an internal Small, Medium or Large slot to add armor to a ship.
- **Global shell scaling:** Armor is applied as an external shell overlay that scales its total material requirements and weight modifiers directly with the **Total Sum of Allocated Module Slots**. A 120-slot *Dreadnought* layout automatically requires ten times more `silicon_carbide` or composite steel to line its frame than a 12-slot *Escort*, ensuring that larger custom designs carry realistic, weight-scaling physical and financial launch tax penalties.

#### Physics and capability validation rules

Before a user can finalize and save a ship design blueprint, the interface runs a multi-variable validation pass. If any of the following constraints are broken, the blueprint is flagged with an engineering error and cannot be queued for manufacturing:

- **The power grid constraint:** The total structural energy draw demanded by active sub-systems (such as sensors, shield grid arrays and automated point-defense networks) cannot exceed the continuous energy output generated by the equipped propulsion drives or dedicated power modules:
- **The manufacturing complexity cap:** The designer checks its components against the owner's available shipyard complexity capacity, with the basic surface capacity as a planning floor before the first yard is built. Queueing a hull requires an operational yard in the selected system that meets the blueprint's captured complexity. An empire-wide nanotechnology research tier remains a future constraint because research currently stores unlocked IDs rather than tier levels.
- **The thrust-to-mass launch barrier:** The interface computes the absolute **Maximum Launch Mass** (the total structural dry mass of all modules and hull plating combined with the maximum weight capacity of all internal cargo holds). It checks this value against the raw thrust yield generated by the ship's propulsion drives:
- **Launch capability error:** If the total thrust output of the selected drives falls below the `Required Minimum Thrust`, the interface flags a critical warning: **the ship is too heavy to blast off from the home world's surface**. The user must either substitute heavy modules for lighter alternatives, upgrade propulsion performance or commit to manufacturing the ship exclusively in orbital space stations.

For an interstellar sublight order, each ship's departure acceleration is its design thrust divided by its dry mass plus carried fuel, cargo and passengers (80 kg per passenger). The slowest ship sets the fleet's trajectory. Recognized main drives have provisional exhaust velocities: chemical rocket, fission thermal, fusion, MPD ion and antimatter. The amount of propellant aboard each ship limits its achievable speed through the rocket equation; half of the available delta-v is reserved for braking. The fleet accelerates, coasts and brakes, with a 0.99c ceiling. Propellant is deducted from each ship during the acceleration and braking portions of daily ticks. A ship with a recognized main drive and no propellant cannot begin a sublight crossing. Designs without a recognized main drive retain the earlier unlimited-fuel profile temporarily until their blueprints are migrated. The ship designer offers only drives supported by the owner's researched technology: rocketry for RP-1/LOX, methane-oxygen propulsion for methane/LOX, hydrogen-oxygen propulsion for hydrogen/LOX, nuclear fission for fission thermal, superconductors for MPD ion, nuclear fusion for fusion and antimatter for antimatter drives. Both blueprint registration and construction orders enforce this gate. A current design may have only one recognized main drive, and it must have a propellant tank. New custom designs include a 15,000 kg tank; tank capacity counts toward the maximum launch mass. Chemical rockets purchase their fuel and liquid oxygen separately and track their combined mass in the tank. Their provisional fuel mass shares are 28% for RP-1, 22% for methane and 17% for hydrogen. Fission thermal and fusion drives use hydrogen gas, MPD drives use methane-derived propellant and antimatter drives use containment cells. A ship may buy its drive's materials from a local surface or station hub and refill up to tank capacity. Refueling in orbit or at a dock above a surface hub also pays for a real surface-to-orbit launch service. Commissioned ships start empty and buy an initial 100 kg if local stock, funds and launch service permit. Fission thermal drives also buy refined uranium or thorium as reactor fuel. Fusion drives use hydrogen reaction mass, with deuterium gas or fusion pellets in cargo improving exhaust speed. Reactor fuel for a planned leg is committed at departure, while propellant burns during daily acceleration and braking. Solid motors remain a separate future booster or launch implementation because the current main-drive model assumes a restartable, throttleable engine. Plutonium fuel, boil-off, mass change during acceleration and relativistic time dilation remain future work. Warp remains a separate researched four-day transit; it no longer consumes sublight propellant, while warp power draw and drive-module performance remain future work.

Local journeys use provisional maneuver budgets of 10 m/s for docking, 30 m/s between orbital sites and 50 m/s for deep-space travel. The rocket equation turns each budget and the ship's loaded mass into a propellant requirement using its researched drive's exhaust velocity. The required propellant and any reactor fuel are committed at departure; journey progress still advances daily over the existing fixed duration. Surface-to-orbit ascent on the same body uses an external launch provider instead of ship propellant. Automated trade routes buy a local fuel shortfall from an accessible hub and book the cost against route profit. A route waits if fuel or money is unavailable. Ships with no recognized drive still retain the legacy free-movement behavior until their designs are migrated. Local travel times do not yet derive from thrust, body separation or orbital mechanics.

#### Departure previews

The fleet page previews an inter-system departure from the current snapshot before submission. It shows local departure days plus crossing days, a year equivalent for long voyages, sublight distance and peak speed, local and crossing propellant budgets including braking, drive reactor fuel, any surface launch charge and electrical readiness. Missing fuel, launch access, passenger supplies, electrical reserves or conflicting orders disable departure. Electrical failures retain a diagnostic preview. Later state changes are checked again when the command executes. Legacy designs identify their electrical endurance as unknown.

##### Current provisional implementation

New player and corporate blueprints freeze their electrical equipment and fuel conversion profiles. The workbench defaults to solar when `electricity` and `solar_power` are researched. Starting scenarios include both. Solar arrays have a 120 kW reference output at Sol and one AU with four slots, 2,500 kg dry mass and complexity 2. A battery provides 500 kWh capacity, 100 kW charging and 200 kW discharge with two slots and 2,500 kg mass. Dedicated chemical reserves hold 15,000 kg in two slots with 2,000 kg tank mass. Electrical reactor fuel storage holds 100 kg in one slot with 500 kg compartment mass. New ships have empty electrical reserves and empty batteries.

Chemical generation uses the mixture coefficients below. Fission electricity provisionally yields 6,000,000 kWh/kg of refined uranium or 5,000,000 kWh/kg of refined thorium. These are separate electrical conversion coefficients and do not consume propulsion reactor fuel or freight. The module's electrical output rating abstracts its converter and radiator limits; separate thermal equipment is not yet modeled. Ships, planetary solar plants and explicit station solar-array modules share `solarFactor = relativeStellarLuminosity / distanceAu^2`. Relative luminosity provisionally follows the galaxy generator's mass/type relation: `(stellarMass / solarMass)^3.5`, with giant and white dwarf adjustments and zero output from black holes. Moon locations use their parent planet's stellar distance. At Sol, the factor is 4 at 0.5 AU, 1 at 1 AU and 0.25 at 2 AU. Reference array output scales by this factor without an artificial lower floor or a cap at its 1 AU rating; thermal and converter limits await later refinement. Planetary plants retain their separate atmospheric attenuation. Station solar arrays use the factor without surface atmospheric attenuation. Local orbital sites use two 10-hour sunlight/2-hour eclipse cycles per day; surface sites use 12 hours of sunlight and 12 hours of darkness. Local transfers use the lower endpoint flux and longer endpoint eclipse. Ships can use panels throughout local space travel and can deploy or stow them without stopping. Unspecified system-space rendezvous locations use the outermost known planetary orbit with a 1 AU minimum; named body locations use their parent orbit and named star sites use one stellar diameter. Atmospheric surface transfers suppress ship solar output and reject deployment for the whole abstract transfer envelope; airless surface transfers remain solar-capable. Stationary surface ships can use panels. Missing stellar/location data and interstellar crossings guarantee no solar output. These conservative envelopes begin in sunlight and are approximations rather than orbit calculations.

Daily accounting splits intervals at sunlight, eclipse, interstellar acceleration, coasting and braking boundaries. Local transfers provisionally charge drive power throughout their one- or two-day maneuver envelope. Cargo holds use 10% standby draw plus occupancy-scaled preservation draw for vulnerable materials. Essential services are supplied before cargo preservation and propulsion. Charging stores 90% of input energy. Paid dock or surface charging withdraws actual energy from local grid storage at 0.02 credits/kWh and credits its owner; grid generation cannot be borrowed from future ticks. Solar surplus charges automatically. Electrical fuel purchases and deployment changes are staged commands. Trade routes buy a conservative local maneuver budget and arrival reserve before departure. Generator supplies add to loaded mass and launch quotes. Departure previews check each ship and retain 48 hours of essential and cargo energy at arrival using destination illumination for known local sites. Long crossing previews evaluate a small set of analytic phases rather than looping over voyage days.

New MPD catalog drives provide 3.9 N at 120 kW and 65% efficiency with 40,000 m/s exhaust velocity. Captured efficiency and actual electricity constrain their thrust. Chemical and nuclear thermal propulsion retain separate thrust models. A power interruption invalidates the controlled itinerary at the first essential or drive supply failure. Every ship stops its burn at that time and conserves unused drive energy. Sublight fleets retain their actual position and velocity along the original crossing, coast through subsequent ticks and can drift beyond the target without claiming arrival. Recovery plans start from current motion and loaded mass, require enough remaining propellant for acceleration and braking and check electricity for the entire replacement itinerary plus arrival reserves. New propulsion reactor fuel is committed separately; earlier committed reactor fuel is not refunded. Recovery consumes main propellant during powered phases and permits arrival only after the powered brake finishes. A second outage preserves the actual replacement trajectory velocity. This provisional model uses one-dimensional motion with fixed acceleration for each itinerary. Interrupted local transfers and warp journeys retain their powered fractional progress and remain paused until a recovery command passes the remaining electrical budget and arrival reserve checks. Local resumption reuses prepaid maneuver propellant, reactor fuel and launch services; any queued crossing must still have its reserved main propellant. Warp resumption requires live warp research and preserves corridor progress rather than restarting the full crossing. Fleets may deploy or stow arrays while traveling in space; stowing can cause a power interruption. Solar charging can restore a local ship while its transfer progress remains fixed. Local position, velocity and warp re-entry physics are abstract; resumption provisionally retains the existing itinerary. Same-owner rescue fleets can intercept a coasting target and match its velocity under the [draft rescue rendezvous rules](RescueRendezvous.md). Gravity, lateral navigation and return after overshoot remain future work. Same-owner stationary ships can transfer actual fuel supplies under the provisional emergency resupply rules below. Passenger outages have a provisional 24-hour grace period followed by 1% conscious or 2% stasis losses per equivalent outage day. Only ongoing outages cause new losses. Cargo power shortfalls now drive provisional material-specific deterioration, with cumulative grace periods and fractional losses. Lost freight is removed from physical stock and route cost is written off without claiming delivery.

Existing designs without a captured electrical profile retain their previous electrical behavior, including new orders, and are explicitly labeled as legacy compatibility mode. They are not filled with invented fuel or battery charge. Retrofitting these designs and migrating their electrical behavior remain future work. ##### Reference examples and propulsion roles

Orion's European Service Module uses four solar-array wings with a total output of about 11.2 kW. NASA describes Gateway's Power and Propulsion Element as a 60 kW solar-powered spacecraft whose electric thrusters consume that power to accelerate xenon propellant. These examples inform the separation of electrical supply and propulsion; their ratings are reference examples rather than game component specifications. [ESA's Orion power description](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Human_and_Robotic_Exploration/Orion/European_Service_Module_Power), [NASA's Gateway power and propulsion description](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/artemis/a-powerhouse-in-deep-space-gateways-power-and-propulsion-element/).

NASA describes nuclear electric propulsion as reactor heat converted into electricity for electric thrusters and nuclear thermal propulsion as reactor heat applied directly to propellant. The game should keep those technologies distinct. NASA's Space Reactor-1 Freedom mission currently targets a late-2028 launch to demonstrate nuclear electric propulsion; this is a planned demonstration rather than an operational capability or guaranteed launch date. [NASA's space nuclear propulsion overview](https://www.nasa.gov/space-technology-mission-directorate/tdm/space-nuclear-propulsion/), [NASA's Space Reactor-1 Freedom mission](https://www.nasa.gov/mission/space-reactor-1-freedom/).

- **Chemical propulsion:** RP-1/LOX, methane/LOX and hydrogen/LOX engines obtain their main propulsion energy from fuel and oxidizer. Their control systems and ship services still need electricity, which may come from solar arrays and batteries.
- **Solar electric propulsion:** Solar arrays supply electricity to an electric drive. The drive also consumes its own compatible reaction mass. Sunlight supplies energy but does not replace propellant.
- **Nuclear electric propulsion:** A fission reactor, heat-to-electricity converter, radiator system and electric drive form an NEP installation. Reactor fuel and thruster propellant are separate supplies. The electrical power source does not itself produce ion thrust.
- **Nuclear thermal propulsion:** Keep the existing fission thermal drive concept separate from NEP. A ship's electrical reactor is not automatically a nuclear thermal main drive.
- **Travel performance:** Calculate propellant requirements and travel time from available power, exhaust velocity, thrust, loaded mass and trajectory. Do not assign a universal 90% fuel discount or a guaranteed faster Mars transfer merely because a ship has nuclear equipment. Higher exhaust velocity can reduce reaction-mass requirements while available electrical power limits thrust.

##### Solar arrays and illumination

- **Research and equipment:** Proposed arrays require `electricity` and the researched `solar_power` application. A starter scenario intended to support solar-powered flight must include those technologies. Arrays have captured deployed area, rated output at a reference irradiance, mass, slots, orientation limits and electrical bus limits. These catalog values still need definition.
- **Distance-dependent output:** Proposed output is `referenceOutputKw * stellarLuminosityRelativeToSun * (1 AU / distanceFromStar)^2 * orientationFraction * visibleSunFraction * conditionFraction`, capped by the array's electrical and thermal operating limits. All fractions are between zero and one. Sum separately modeled contributions from multiple stars. A valid distance and stellar luminosity are required; missing data does not justify full rated output.
- **Reference rating:** The rated output describes deployed arrays at one AU from a Sun-like star under reference conditions. It is not guaranteed at every planet or throughout a voyage. Under the proposed idealized distance rule, a 60 kW reference array produces about 26.7 kW at 1.5 AU before other losses and operating limits.
- **Eclipses and deployment:** A fully shaded, stowed or destroyed array produces no electricity. Planetary and lunar eclipses require stored energy or another source. Track deployment and damage in ship state; an undeployed array cannot provide full power during a launch or maneuver.
- **Voyage illumination:** Forecast sunlight along the itinerary, including arrival orbit and scheduled burns. A lunar orbit uses distance from the star for irradiance and local geometry for eclipses. System-to-system galactic coordinates alone cannot define local solar output. If detailed eclipse geometry is unavailable, use an explicit conservative catalog envelope and identify that approximation in the preview.
- **Deep space:** Solar power declines as the ship moves away from contributing stars. Being technically inside a system does not establish adequate output. Interstellar travel must be evaluated along its trajectory and cannot assume departure illumination persists throughout the crossing.
- **Surface conditions:** Atmospheric attenuation and dust-storm losses apply to surface arrays where modeled. Orbiting arrays use their orbital illumination and eclipse conditions; surface weather must not automatically reduce their output.

Sunlight adds no consumable fuel requirement. Endurance still depends on sufficient instantaneous output, battery coverage for dark intervals, equipment condition and any backup fuel. In the initial draft, array output stays constant for a fixed modeled environment between recorded damage or maintenance events; long-term degradation needs a separate lifetime rule before reliable multi-year endurance is claimed.

##### Power generation and fuel

- **Chemical generation:** Retain the current 500 kW auxiliary generator rating as a provisional game value for an optional backup or alternative. This rating is not inferred from Orion or Gateway. Fuel consumption follows electricity actually produced. An idle generator produces no electricity and burns no fuel in the initial model. Startup costs and minimum operating loads can be added later.
- **Supported mixtures:** The generator may use RP-1/LOX, methane/LOX or hydrogen/LOX. Liquid oxygen is an oxidizer required alongside the selected fuel. The player selects a compatible mixture while resupplying; the generator cannot switch to an unavailable mixture during a journey. Nuclear research is not required.
- **Separate supplies:** Propulsion propellant and generator supplies are separately recorded. Purchasing propellant into the main drive tank does not also fill the generator. A transfer between compatible compartments must be an explicit command. The same kilogram cannot be reserved or consumed by both systems.
- **Fission generation:** Fission reactors require `nuclear_fission` research and consume a separately recorded compatible reactor fuel. Their energy yield, minimum load and startup reserve must come from a power-source catalog. Propulsion reactor-fuel ratios must not be reused as electrical-generation rates. These fission coefficients remain to be defined; nuclear electricity must not be treated as unlimited.
- **Captured properties:** A blueprint captures generator output limits, conversion coefficients, storage limits and module power profiles. Research changes affect new or refitted designs. A higher output rating does not automatically improve fuel efficiency.

Proposed chemical conversion coefficients apply to kilograms of fuel, excluding the oxidizer. They represent net electrical output after generator losses and are game balance parameters rather than engineering specifications:

| Mixture | Fuel share of combined mass | Net electricity per kg of fuel | Net electricity per kg of combined mixture |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RP-1/LOX | 28% | 3.6 kWh | 1.008 kWh |
| Methane/LOX | 22% | 4.2 kWh | 0.924 kWh |
| Hydrogen/LOX | 17% | 10.0 kWh | 1.700 kWh |

For an interval of `hours`, electrical demand is `loadKw * hours`. Generator electricity cannot exceed `ratedOutputKw * hours`. If a generator supplies `generatedKwh`, its mixture consumption is `generatedKwh / electricityPerKgMixture`; debit fuel and oxidizer according to their mass shares. Available output is limited by whichever ingredient runs out first. Partially available stock permits only the corresponding partial generation.

##### Electric drive power and thrust

Draft: Electric propulsion receives only the electrical power left after required ship services and protected charging commitments. A proposed first model uses `thrustN = 2 * thrusterEfficiency * propulsionPowerW / exhaustVelocityMps`, with efficiency between zero and one, consistent units and separate hardware limits. Its compatible propellant consumption is `thrustN / exhaustVelocityMps` in kg/s. Converter and radiator limits constrain usable reactor electricity; reactor thermal output must not be treated as electrical output.

An electric drive receiving zero electricity provides zero thrust. Reduced solar illumination or a reactor fault reduces available propulsion power and requires a corresponding trajectory calculation. Solar-powered and nuclear-powered versions may use the same electric drive while differing in their supply profile. Higher exhaust velocity at a fixed power level reduces thrust, so fuel economy and transfer time must be evaluated together. The current MPD drive's fixed thrust and power ratings remain provisional placeholders and must be reconciled with this relationship before power-dependent trajectories are enabled. Chemical main-drive thrust follows its separate combustion and propellant model.

##### Storage and resupply

- **Generator reserves:** Record actual material quantities and the capacity of their supported storage compartments. Both fuel and oxidizer count toward loaded ship mass. Compartments require compatible materials; empty storage provides no energy. Tank mass, slots, capacities and cryogenic handling requirements need catalog definitions before implementation.
- **Cargo accounting:** Materials allocated to a dedicated generator tank are removed from ordinary cargo. Materials remaining in cargo still occupy cargo capacity and add to loaded mass. A generator consumes from its allocated compartment; it cannot silently drain freight or reserved propulsion supplies.
- **Batteries:** Record usable capacity in kWh, stored charge and maximum charge/discharge power. The initial draft uses 90% charging efficiency: consuming 100 kWh adds at most 90 kWh of stored charge. Discharging reduces stored charge by delivered electricity. Battery mass, slots and capacity need catalog definitions. New batteries start empty.
- **Paid resupply:** Commands validate local access, compatible stock, storage capacity, owner funds and any required launch service. Fuel and oxidizer purchases are atomic. Docked charging uses a real local power source, available generation and its electricity bill; a powered station does not grant free electricity. A ship may also charge from its own solar surplus without buying sunlight. That charging uses real available output, charge-rate limits and the same conversion losses.
- **Mass consistency:** Generator supplies add to launch mass and itinerary calculations. Burning them reduces current loaded mass. Replanning must retain previously consumed fuel and committed braking reserves. Relativistic effects and boil-off remain separate future rules.

##### Loads during travel

Module catalogs should specify active, standby and disabled loads. The existing single `powerDrawKw` is insufficient to calculate endurance across an entire voyage. Proposed opening profiles are:

| Consumer | Active load | Coasting or standby load |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ship control, navigation and housekeeping | 2 kW per ship | 2 kW while the ship is operational |
| Main drive electrical systems | Captured drive power draw during maneuvering, acceleration and braking | 0 kW during an unpowered coast |
| Pressurized cargo vault | Current 30 kW rating during cargo operations | 3 kW while maintaining ordinary stored cargo |
| Conscious passenger life support | 1 kW per embarked passenger | Remains required throughout the voyage |
| Occupied stasis pod | Current 80 kW rating per occupied pod | Remains required throughout stasis |

- **Coasting:** Turning off the main drive removes its electrical draw but preserves control, required cargo services and passenger loads. Coasting does not provide free electricity.
- **Empty systems:** Empty passenger facilities and empty stasis pods may be disabled. Stasis reduces nutrient consumption but still requires electricity. Passenger counts must come from actual ship occupants and manifests. Occupied pod counts must respect captured seat capacities.
- **Optional systems:** Weapons, active sensors, mining tools and construction equipment consume their active load only when used. Their standby profiles require catalog entries. A freight contract may impose an uninterrupted refrigeration load; it cannot be disabled while claiming compliant delivery.
- **Load shedding:** Proposed priority is ship control and navigation, occupied life support and stasis, required cargo preservation, drive electrical demand then optional equipment. Reserve power must not be spent on discretionary tasks. If drive power is unavailable, thrust-based acceleration or braking cannot proceed as planned.

##### Departure checks and reserves

Draft: Integrate electrical demand separately over local departure, acceleration, coasting, braking and arrival. Use the same phase durations and power profiles in the preview and the simulation. Do not multiply peak drive load by the full crossing duration or assume a stasis voyage has zero load.

- **Availability:** Check output limits as well as total energy. Enough stored energy cannot compensate for inadequate instantaneous array, generator or battery output during a required phase. Battery state of charge must remain sufficient at every point in a forecast eclipse; a positive daily solar average does not establish coverage for the darkest interval.
- **Trip supplies:** Reserve the forecast generator ingredients, battery charge, propulsion budget, drive reactor fuel and passenger supplies without double counting shared material stocks. Fleet readiness follows the least capable ship; energy cannot be shared between ships without a defined transfer capability.
- **Arrival reserve:** Proposed minimum is energy for two additional days of essential arrival loads. Keep any planned propulsion braking reserve separate. Essential arrival loads include control, occupied life support and required cargo preservation; the reserve must have sufficient output capability as well as energy.
- **Feedback:** Show power sources, expected solar output and its reference conditions, eclipse duration, peak phase demand, power allocated to electric propulsion, phase energy totals, required and available generator supplies, battery charge, arrival reserve and estimated endurance. Explain which ship and resource block departure. Missing catalog data means endurance is unknown and cannot be presented as a confirmed capability.
- **Live validation:** Recheck against the latest tick state when executing a departure command. Loading passengers or freight after a preview can change mass, power demand and endurance. Passenger and cargo changes must respect reservations for an active itinerary.

Example using the proposed coefficients: A continuous 50 kW load requires 1,200 kWh per day. RP-1/LOX generation therefore consumes about 1,190 kg of combined mixture per day. A dedicated 15,000 kg generator reserve supports about 12.6 days at that load before an arrival reserve is withheld. This example is separate from the existing 15,000 kg propulsion tank and does not introduce a free generator tank.

Solar example: A 60 kW array supplying a continuous 50 kW load has 10 kW of charging surplus while fully illuminated under its reference conditions. A two-hour eclipse requires 100 kWh of usable battery charge for that load. Replacing that charge at 90% charging efficiency requires about 111.1 kWh of surplus generation, or 11.1 hours at the stated surplus. Repeated eclipses therefore require checking both battery capacity and whether charge recovers between them. At the idealized 1.5 AU output above, that same array cannot continuously support the 50 kW load without additional generation or a limited battery discharge.

Chemical propulsion remains eligible for journeys whose propulsion and electrical budgets can actually be supplied. A workable local trip does not establish endurance for a multi-year crossing. Long chemical-powered inter-system voyages may require much larger stores, lower operating loads or another researched power source. Travel distance or time must not be silently shortened to make an under-supplied ship pass validation.

##### Daily processing, depletion and persistence

- **Tick accounting:** Only the coordinated engine tick consumes fuel and charge. Integrate partial-day phase boundaries so the day containing both a burn and a coast is charged for the correct hours. UI calculations remain cosmetic. Docked ships consume local power when a paid connection is available or use onboard stores otherwise.
- **Generation and charge:** Supply required loads from an authorized local connection where available, then deployed solar arrays, scheduled onboard generators and batteries. Solar surplus may charge batteries under the selected charging policy. Additional fueled-generator output may charge batteries only when explicitly scheduled and must consume fuel and account for charging losses. Avoid charging and discharging the same battery simultaneously. Energy, fuel and charge must remain finite and nonnegative.
- **Power failure:** Shed optional loads first. Preserve position, elapsed time, fuel and charge already consumed. An unpowered ship can continue ballistic coasting but cannot receive free acceleration, braking or a successful controlled arrival. Mark its itinerary as unable to complete and require a revised feasible plan or assistance.
- **Passengers and cargo:** A life-support or stasis shortfall records unmet electricity and time without service. Daily survival and cargo deterioration need explicit grace periods and loss rates before this feature is enabled. Stasis must not grant immunity to power loss and the first shortage must not silently delete all passengers.
- **Saved state:** Persist fuel compartments, selected generator mixture, battery charge, captured profiles, itinerary reservations and outstanding shortfalls. Reloading must not refill stores, reset a failure or charge the same interval twice.
- **Legacy saves:** Missing power-store data is unknown endurance rather than a full tank or battery. A migration must identify compatible equipment and require paid resupply before accepting a newly planned journey. How already active legacy journeys finish needs an explicit compatibility rule before release; do not silently strand them during loading.

##### Emergency ship resupply (provisional implementation)

Fleet ship cards can stage `TransferShipSuppliesCommand` to transfer supplies between different ships with the same owner in the same system and at the same stationary site. Transfers are instantaneous and lossless at the next tick; pumping rates, equipment requirements and transfer losses await tuning. Named sites must exist. Generic system-space sites use the current abstract colocation model.

- Electrical fuel can come from donor cargo or a matching dedicated electrical fuel compartment. Chemical transfers require both fuel and oxidizer in the receiver's captured mixture ratio. Reactor transfers use the selected compatible refined feed. Quantities represent total mixture mass and occupy the receiver's dedicated generator or reactor tank.
- Main-drive propellant can come from cargo ingredients or a compatible donor propellant tank. Drive reactor material moves from donor cargo to receiver cargo and counts against its freight capacity. Propellant transfers do not automatically supply nuclear drive reactor feed; readiness checks still require it where applicable.
- Missing stock, incompatible feeds, mixing nonempty electrical compartments or exceeding capacity reject the whole transfer. Donor stocks decrease by exactly the supplied material quantities. No market, treasury or battery energy is created or consumed by the transfer.
- Transfers preserve passengers, power shortfalls, charge counters and travel commitments. A fleet interrupted at zero local progress is still at its departure site and can receive supplies. Recovery remains a separate command that checks the remaining journey and arrival reserves. Sublight drift can receive supplies after a velocity-matched intercept under the [draft rescue rendezvous rules](RescueRendezvous.md). Partway local transfers and warp rescues remain future work.
- Existing cargo, propulsion and electrical reserve fields persist the result through save/load without a new save format.

##### Cargo preservation (provisional implementation)

Captured electrical designs with a positive cargo preservation load track equivalent unpowered hours for each vulnerable material pool. Each tick adds `unmetCargoKwh / cargoKw`, bounded to 24 hours. A partial shortage proportionally increases exposure. Stable or unlisted materials are unaffected. Legacy electrical designs and holds without a modeled cargo load retain their previous behavior.

| Cargo material | Grace period | Loss per equivalent unpowered day after grace |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Food stocks (`food_matrix`) | 24 hours | 5% |
| Agricultural biomass | 48 hours | 2% |
| Liquid oxygen | 6 hours | 2% |
| Liquid methane | 6 hours | 2% |
| Liquid hydrogen | 6 hours | 3% |

Losses compound fractionally as `remainingKg = initialKg * (1 - dailyLossFraction)^(exposedHours / 24)`. Grace is cumulative across outages while the material remains aboard. Restored power stops further losses and never restores lost stock or resets its exposure. New stock mixed into an existing pool shares its exposure; an empty pool clears its history on the next tick. Batch quality and unloading/reloading within one tick await refinement.

Losses apply only to freight in `storedCargoKg`. Propulsion tanks, electrical fuel compartments, battery charge and stable reactor materials are unaffected. Spoiled or vented mass leaves the usable cargo manifest without creating saleable waste. Tick processing applies deterioration to its final electrical result, including any stopped-flight replay; departure previews never consume cargo or exposure grace.

Freight routes reconcile their recorded cargo against the carrier's actual stock before delivery. Missing mass reduces outstanding cargo cost proportionally and records an operating loss without another cash payment. Only actual delivered mass counts as volume moved. Fully lost shipments return rather than waiting indefinitely to sell absent cargo. These rules also reconcile missing freight consumed or removed by other modeled operations.

Save version 30 persists cargo exposure and last-tick losses alongside electrical state. Older saves begin with empty exposure history and retain their actual cargo and reserves. Ship power cards show per-material exposure hours and losses for the last tick. These coefficients are provisional gameplay rules; thermal conditions, packaging, material-specific cooling loads, waste recovery and batch quality await tuning.

##### Remaining tuning

The current arrival check covers 48 hours of essential and cargo demand. Known local destinations use their own stellar illumination and eclipse envelope, beginning with a full night or eclipse so the forecast never relies on immediate sunlight. Interstellar and unknown arrivals retain a dark reserve. Atmospheric travel remains solar-free; a stationary ship after landing may use surface sunlight but must survive its 12-hour night. Reserve forecasts retain battery capacity, charge/discharge rates, 90% charging efficiency and daily charge limits and never alter physical supplies.

Stasis demand now has 2 kW overhead per occupied pod plus 78 kW multiplied by the fraction of a pod's captured seats occupied. A standard full 100-seat pod still draws 80 kW; 10 occupants draw 9.8 kW and one occupant draws 2.78 kW. Empty pods remain off. Captured seat capacity controls the fraction for refined designs. These runtime coefficients apply to existing captured electrical designs too; captured module ratings and physical reserves are unchanged.

Cargo demand is `ratedCargoKw * (0.1 + 0.9 * utilization)`, where utilization is weighted vulnerable mass divided by captured cargo capacity, bounded to 0–1. Food weight is 1, biomass 0.5 and cryogenic liquids 2. Stable or unlisted freight receives no preservation weight but the hold still draws 10% standby power. A standard 30 kW, 30,000 kg vault draws 3.9 kW for 1,000 kg of food, 4.8 kW for 1,000 kg of cryogenic liquid and at most 30 kW. If a design lacks positive cargo capacity, carried vulnerable stock conservatively uses the full captured draw. No new save fields or free energy are introduced by these provisional tuning changes.

The repeatable [ship endurance balance report](ShipEnduranceReport.md) compares validated catalog blueprints with tick results. It separates journey completion from the conservative arrival reserve, contrasts empty and loaded holds and compares conscious and stasis occupancy with destination-aware local reserves. Its 1-light-year cases are analytic forecasts; short synthetic crossings verify tick agreement without presenting them as realistic stellar distances. The report documents the current provisional adjustments; executing the audit changes no production game state.

Tune the provisional array, battery, tank, fuel yield, thruster, passive-load, outage and arrival-reserve values above. Refine stellar luminosities, local geometry, deployment and damage limits, converter and radiator equipment, degradation, stasis recovery, cargo preservation tuning, evolving-mass trajectories and legacy refits. Fuel cells, shared propulsion/generator tanks and inter-ship energy transfer require additional rules if included. Coverage should continue verifying distance-scaled solar output, eclipses and recharge, deployment, mixed-phase energy use, atomic purchases, reserve enforcement, coasting, power-limited electric thrust, power failure and uninterrupted save/load accounting.

#### Fleet encounters (provisional)

After daily movement and passenger arrival, idle fleets sharing a physical site in the same system automatically fight when their empires are at `TOTAL_WAR`. A fleet can take part in one encounter per turn. The encounter resolver applies surviving ship instances to the game state, removes fleets with no surviving ships and records compact battle history. Battles in transit and encounters outside total war are skipped. The resolver currently delegates to `TacticalCombatProcessor` behind `FleetEncounterResolver`; this adapter is temporary because tactical combat is planned for a complete overhaul. Battle history is saved and retains the latest 1,000 encounters.

#### Cost and work estimate

Registration and editing feedback uses the command queue's explicit execution receipt. A submission stays pending until processed or cancelled, even if other snapshots arrive. Rejected edits cannot appear successful merely because an unchanged blueprint is already present. The next snapshot refresh reads completed receipts without blocking the UI thread. These receipts are transient control-layer feedback and are not saved as game state.

The designer previews the same material bill and work requirement used when a ship build order is queued. These values update as hull material, armor, thickness, propulsion or optional modules change.

- **Work unit:** One standardized shipyard labor-hour. Base required work is `max(200, dry mass in kg / 100)` and the blueprint's captured manufacturing multiplier applies afterward. A tier 1 surface yard contributes 100 base work-hours per simulation day. This is a first-pass mass-based measure; later balancing can refine component receipts and production work.
- **Material bill:** The current generic bill uses the greater of 1,000 kg or the design's dry mass. It allocates 65% to hull material, 20% to armor material, 10% to refined copper and 5% to silicon. Each cryogenic stasis pod adds 100 kg refined aluminum and 50 kg refined copper. These are estimated input quantities, not a component-level bill of materials.
- **Completion estimate:** The displayed turn count is the minimum at the current 100 work-hours per day and assumes materials are available. Shortages, affordability and yard capacity can delay actual completion.
- **Naval hub readiness:** Before queuing, the fleet naval hub compares each required material with supply at the selected system's actual shipyard hub, displays current unit prices and estimates the full bill cost against the empire treasury. Orders may still be queued with shortages, but work can pause until materials or funds become available. Missing market quotes are called out instead of treated as a zero price.
- **Corporate fleet access:** Corporations may evaluate eligible public blueprints for **Cargo Transport** or **Mine Ship** roles. A proprietary blueprint remains available only to its owning corporation, even when its design record appears in a shared registry.

#### Construction scheduling and progress (Draft)

Saving a valid blueprint does not create a ship. Commissioning a hull creates a construction order at a compatible shipyard and ties that order to the blueprint version, owner, yard and bill of materials. A yard must meet the design's size and complexity limits. Only one hull may occupy a slipway at a time; additional orders wait in that yard's queue.

- **Shared time base:** One simulation turn is one game day. A shipyard adds work only on a daily turn, regardless of the selected display speed. Even a small hull finishes no earlier than its first eligible daily turn. A ship that requires several days retains its accumulated work between turns, saves and loads.
- **Effective daily work:** Available `engineer` and `industrial_worker` hours, powered slipway capacity and researched assembly efficiency determine how many work hours the yard can contribute that day. Work cannot exceed the yard's daily capacity or the order's remaining work. The exact shift length and efficiency coefficients need balancing.
- **Progress calculation:** The order records required work hours and accumulated work hours. At each daily turn, `accumulatedWorkHours = min(requiredWorkHours, accumulatedWorkHours + effectiveWorkHoursForDay)`. The UI shows completed work, total work, percentage and an estimated completion date based on the current effective rate. If the rate is zero, the estimate is unavailable rather than implying instant completion.
- **Blocking and resources:** Available materials and owner cash support partial daily work; the order pauses when it cannot buy any more of its remaining bill. Orbital construction can consume the required materials from an owner transport docked at that station when local hub stock is insufficient. The intended yard rules will also limit work by qualified workers, power and slipway access. Material purchases are already settled against local stock, while reservations and cancellation refund rules remain Draft.
- **Completion:** The ship is instantiated and assigned to its owner only after required work and material obligations are satisfied. A player purchase from a corporate shipyard transfers a finished ship; it does not grant access to the corporation's proprietary blueprint or let the player place a construction order from it.

The current `ShipDesign` record stores an owner ID and a proprietary flag. `QueueShipBuildCommand` rejects corporation-owned blueprints and creates a daily construction order using the shared `ShipConstructionRequirements` estimate. The order stores standardized work units, the generic mass-based material bill and purchased material progress. Partial local supply allows work to start; missing inputs or owner cash pause progress. A ship is commissioned only when both work and the full bill are complete. Corporate orders use proprietary designs but cannot be placed by the player command. Component-level receipts and finished corporate-yard purchases are not yet implemented.

#### Yard work capacity (initial model)

Recognized main drives now link to their research applications. New player and corporate blueprints apply the selected path's thrust and complexity modifiers. Their manufacturing multiplier weights the drive's material/work cost change by its share of total blueprint dry mass, so the hull's entire bill does not receive a drive-only upgrade penalty. These values are captured in the blueprint and reused by readiness previews and construction orders. Later path changes affect newly created or edited blueprints. Corporate procurement retains its provisional hull template, selects a researched chemical or fission drive and reuses existing proprietary blueprints without changing their captured values. This remains a provisional aggregate bill rather than a full component receipt model.

The designer defaults to solar arrays when their research is available and otherwise a chemical auxiliary generator. The generator's provisional baseline is 500 kW, four slots, 4,500 kg and complexity 2. It now burns separately purchased electrical supplies on designs with a captured power profile. The designer also offers a fission reactor once `nuclear_fission` is researched. Registration, editing and player construction orders enforce this requirement. Existing built ships retain their saved stats. The fission reactor links to `fission_reactors`; its selected path adjusts generated power, component complexity and its mass-weighted contribution to build costs. Its baseline remains 500 kW, four slots, 3,000 kg and complexity 2. Component stats and generic application catalog values remain separate provisional models.

Early rocket lifecycle coverage checks RP-1/LOX, methane/LOX and hydrogen/LOX ships through blueprint registration, funded orbital construction, separate fuel and oxidizer purchases, a sublight order and arrival after save/load. Missing oxidizer rejects refueling without partial loading or spending. Daily arrival tests use a deliberately short synthetic separation; a separate one-light-year plan confirms that chemical interstellar travel remains extremely slow and requires no nuclear reactor fuel. This coverage does not establish electrical endurance for a real interstellar voyage.

Cargo vaults and corporate large cargo holds link to the provisional `pressurized_cargo_holds` application. Performance increases cargo capacity; miniaturization retains current capacity. Stasis pods apply `cryogenic_stasis_pod` modifiers to their supported passengers, complexity and construction costs. New blueprints freeze the resulting capacities so later application changes affect new or edited designs. Passenger operations enforce captured stasis capacity; old blueprints without that field retain 100 passengers per equipped pod. Blueprint manufacturing metadata includes the captured stasis capacity for save compatibility.

Manufacturing complexity limits come from `shipyard_manufacturing_limits.json`: surface yards support 5 at tier 1 and gain one per additional tier, orbital grids support 7 and capital slipways support 10. Yard selection skips sites below the required complexity. Daily construction pauses if the selected yard loses sufficient capacity. Older blueprints without manufacturing metadata retain baseline costs and derive complexity from recognized drives and stasis pods. Other legacy module IDs still lack production catalog entries.

Daily work now depends on the selected yard and is recalculated from current station and household data. The naval hub shows the same profile used by the construction processor.

- **Yard type:** A completed, dedicated `surface_shipyard` facility contributes 100 base work-hours per day at tier 1. Facility tiers scale its capacity by 1.5 per tier. During a tier upgrade, the yard remains available at 50% capacity. Each online orbital shipyard grid contributes 100 and each online capital slipway contributes 300. An online component assembly module adds 50. A station must be operational and have an online shipyard grid or capital slipway to accept an order. Commercial hubs are separate facilities: a surface shipyard needs a hub on its body to buy construction materials but the hub itself provides no shipbuilding capacity.
- **Staffing:** Orbital yard modules contribute their declared `workforceProfessionId` and `requiredWorkers`. If no worker count is declared, defaults are 100 per shipyard grid, 300 per capital slipway and 50 per component assembly module. Online orbital yard modules post those worker requirements to the station's local labor market; the station owner pays hired workers from its treasury or corporate reserves. Paid headcount and daily wage cost persist on each module. A surface shipyard uses its facility's persistent worker allocation against a full-staff target of 100 workers in its selected profession. The industrial facilities view lets the owner set that allocation. Actual hires paid through the daily payroll determine surface-yard staffing and output; assigned job slots alone do not staff the yard. The staffing model caps allocations against local working-age population after public employment and other facility job allocations are reserved. The lowest filled profession ratio scales yard output; an unstaffed yard makes no progress.
- **Technology:** `automated_assembly_lines` increases output by 25% and reduces required staff by 25%. `cybernetic_workforce_integration` reduces required staff by 25%. These application IDs are read from the owner's unlocked technology list and combine multiplicatively for staffing demand. Orbital modules use the adjusted worker requirement for both payroll job postings and yard capacity.
- **Rate and estimate:** `effective work per day = base work per day × technology output multiplier × staffing fraction`. Surface-yard staffing uses workers hired and paid in the current payroll; those hires are recorded in the facility's persistent industry account. Orbital yard staffing uses paid module headcounts saved on the station module. The same payroll result drives that day's construction work. The industrial facilities and naval hub views show actual paid headcount and daily wage cost alongside assigned jobs. The naval hub computes the minimum days from the current effective rate. An offline or fully unstaffed yard has no completion estimate. Changing staffing, modules, power state or technology changes daily output and the displayed estimate.
- **Yard staffing status:** The industrial facilities view shows surface yard construction and tier-upgrade progress, allocated job slots versus paid workers, wage cost and the maximum supported allocation. The naval hub shows each orbital yard module's paid headcount, requirement, wage cost and online state alongside aggregate staffing and effective capacity. Surface owners can set their persistent worker allocation, which cannot exceed the workforce left after public jobs and other facility allocations. Ship readiness shows yard tier, capacity, paid staffing percentages and effective work rate with the build time estimate.
- **Current limits:** Surface shipyards are built as their own `surface_shipyard` industrial facility and become available at tier 1 after construction completes. The same body needs a commercial hub as its material market. Orders at the same yard share its daily capacity in queue order; a material-blocked order passes unused work to the next order. Worker allocation persists between turns and constrains hiring alongside public employment and other facility jobs, but workers are paid through daily payroll rather than reserved as employees between turns.
- **Shipyard integration coverage:** The control-layer lifecycle test checks that rocketry and space station technology gate station deployment, an owner cargo ship takes a timed local trip to the orbital yard and delivers bill materials after docking and a ship order advances only after the yard module has paid workers. Taking the module offline pauses further work. Payroll tests also confirm that a low treasury or small station workforce reduces paid headcount and scales daily shipbuilding capacity accordingly, while restored funds allow the yard to hire again. The two workforce technologies reduce posted orbital jobs and wages while preserving full capacity at the lower staffing target; fractional staffing reductions are rounded per module consistently for payroll and capacity. Construction tests verify that queued orders share capacity in order and a surface order blocked on an unavailable material passes its work to the next order. Save/load coverage verifies module headcount, wage costs, yard capacity and in-progress order work and material receipts survive reload, then resumes work through a real engine turn. Older saves without module payroll fields load those values as zero.
