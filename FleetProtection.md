# Fleet protection decisions

## Threat assessment

Guards and stationary or moving escorted convoys assess current detected wartime threats before the daily power, movement and combat steps. FleetThreatAssessment consumes live installed sensor coverage, including authorized corporate and diplomatic contributors. Remembered contacts sustain safety priorities and delay return but do not authorize interception or new escape trajectory planning. Revoked grants provide no new detections. Sharing sensor data does not make another owner's ships available as reinforcements.

The initial local threat radius is 100,000 km. Immediately available friendly support consists of own stationary fleets at the protected fleet's local site or moving fleets sharing its actual position within one meter, velocity within 0.001 m/s and gravity frame. Moving and stationary forces do not count as the same group merely because their site labels agree. Distant reinforcements and unrelated journeys are excluded. This conservative support rule avoids assuming that detected nearby ships can reach a fight before it starts. Escort ships already joined to a convoy count once as part of that fleet.

The provisional strength estimate is the square root of combined available firepower times effective remaining endurance. ShipCombatPower quotes the next 60-second salvo from each ship's actual generator fuel, battery energy, output and discharge limits after essential and cargo upkeep. Only funded firing contributes offensive strength. ShipCombatStats supplies current hull condition, active shields and armor deflection. Stance modifiers are shared while random firing variance and range brackets are excluded. Endurance retains current protection without counting future shield recharge or repair. Repeated quotes spend no stores and stage no orders. Unarmed ships gain no firepower from mass or role and shield charge cannot exceed installed emitter capacity. The quote covers the next round rather than promising sustained combat; legacy electrical compatibility remains unchanged. Weapon-specific effectiveness and incomplete enemy identification await the corresponding tactical and sensor mechanisms. See [CombatModules.md](CombatModules.md).

Detected hostile strength greater than twice available friendly strength selects an escape attempt. Otherwise a stationary guard or idle escort may attempt a funded defensive intercept. If its own departing military ships cannot match all detected local hostile strength, it holds its assignment even when nearby friendly support makes escape unnecessary. The ratio and radius are initial policy values, not balance conclusions. This assessment is the decision gate for protection responses; it does not override an explicitly commanded intercept.

## Defensive interception

### Return and fuel recovery decisions

Defensive pursuit retains the original guard object or protected convoy so surviving ships can return and resume protection. Guards return only while their object remains owned and valid. Escorts must physically rendezvous before rejoining, including when their protected fleet has moved. Return and rendezvous require real fuel, compatible drive feed and electrical readiness.

If the fleet cannot fund its return, it first checks whether compatible fuel sharing among its own ships can help. Sharing conserves actual material and protects donors' ability to travel; it cannot manufacture fuel or take paid freight. If sharing is insufficient, splitting an escort or guard fleet is allowed. Ships that can fund the return may proceed while underfueled ships retain the protection assignment and seek recovery independently.

Underfueled ships choose the closest safe owned base with compatible available fuel that they can physically reach and a funded return after buying supplies. Refueling remains a paid transaction bounded by actual stock, capacity and owner funds. If no suitable base is reachable, they request a nearby idle owned fuel transport with compatible dedicated supplies and a transfer pump. A transport must fund its physical rendezvous and its return after delivery. Remote requests do not instantly transfer fuel. Ships unable to reach a base or secure a delivery wait with their physical state and saved assignment intact.

FleetProtectionReturn runs on the simulation tick after ordinary pursuit updates. FleetReturnFuel can pool compatible surplus from several donors while preserving each donor's funded return. FleetFuelRecovery selects reachable ports or physical transport delivery. Save version 55 retains the original assignment and pursuit, return, refueling or supplying stage in FleetInterceptOrder. Older explicit intercept orders have no protection assignment. Canceling automatic pursuit retains return intent; canceling thrust preserves actual ballistic motion and spent fuel.

Return uses same-system local travel. Returning escorts can approach supported moving convoys through the existing scalar, body-frame and native stellar rendezvous planners. FleetEscortReturn validates common ownership and forecasts both the paid approach and the escort's ability to follow the convoy afterward. A bounded preview can retain more approach fuel when the fastest intercept would leave too little for later burns. Fuel sharing, recovery splits and refueling use this complete return requirement.

A coasting escort can also rendezvous with an owned convoy parked in a planetary or lunar body orbit. BodyFleetRendezvous forecasts the convoy's actual parking ephemeris and starts finite burns from the escort's saved position and velocity. Distant same-radius phases can use the existing OrbitalPhasing fallback. Every accepted solution must still match position and velocity at the coordinated movement tick. A shared altitude alone never authorizes merging a coasting escort.

At verified parked contact, EscortFlightContinuation checks arrival electrical reserves then adopts the convoy's unchanged parking location for ordinary fleet transfer. No future convoy burns are charged for an idle convoy. Its original ships and stores remain unchanged by reattachment and the escort retains its spent approach fuel and saved identity. An empty tank, incompatible body frame or unfundable plan preserves actual coast and return intent. A convoy that moves away before contact prevents merging; the paid missed approach becomes actual drift for supported replanning. Existing station docking, ordinary stationary returns and cross-system waiting retain their current paths. These changes reuse save version 56.

Reattachment occurs after the coordinated power and movement tick, only when actual position agrees within one meter and velocity within 0.001 m/s in the same gravity frame. Friendly physical contact does not authorize combat or require hostile sensor coverage. EscortFlightContinuation quotes each escort's own unexecuted propulsion and electrical requirements, commits its remaining drive feed and joins the convoy's unchanged trajectory through normal fleet organization. The convoy keeps its own cargo, ships, flight progress and original maneuver allocations. Completed approach burns are never refunded or charged again.

A changed convoy course can cause a missed rendezvous. The escort retains spent fuel, actual ballistic motion and saved return intent. Supported body coasts and gravity-free drift can attempt a new funded approach from that state. Other motion waits for supported recovery or a stationary convoy. Cross-system returns and native itineraries with a subsequent saved docking approach remain unsupported for moving reattachment. Sensor-sharing agreements do not grant access to foreign fuel transports or bases. Guard return after coordinated escape uses the additional safety gate below.

ProtectionReturnTest checks conserved sharing, pooled donors, insufficient-fuel splitting, paid base refueling, physical transport delivery, moved-convoy reattachment and save/load. A real engine tick verifies that purchases retain both hub stock withdrawal and owner payment. These checks verify mechanisms without establishing balance targets.

MovingEscortReturnTest adds continuing and drifting convoy contact, actual later braking expenditure, a missed approach followed by funded replanning, fuel sharing and recovery splitting, Earth and Moon coasts, owned native stellar maneuver budgets and a real engine tick. Moving approaches and return intent persist using save version 55 without a new schema.

FleetProtectionIntercept previews approaches to up to eight current wartime contacts within the threat radius, ordered by actual distance then fleet ID. Detection uses the common empire sensor network with authorized corporate and diplomatic contributors. Foreign shared hardware provides knowledge without contributing military strength or blueprints. A nearest unfundable target permits trying the next detected target; a target already at the same stationary site remains an ordinary local encounter.

The responding ships must have owned military blueprints, no passenger or supply commitments and actual fuel, drive feed and electrical readiness. Ordinary FleetInterceptProcessor planning provides stationary site approaches and supported moving rendezvous with actual paid burns. An approach requiring a future launch window does not qualify for immediate protection. Preview is immutable; failed funding leaves guard orders, escort membership, cargo and stores intact.

A committing guard relinquishes its fixed-site assignment and persists an intercept order with its original protection assignment. An idle escort can detach through the existing fleet split using its saved identity, leaving protected ships at their actual site and restoring their prior stance. Active or unfinished freight, passengers and scheduled supplies keep escorts with their convoy. Moving convoys also retain their protection and can use the existing coordinated escape path when overwhelmed. Surviving automatic interceptors retain return intent after contact or lost pursuit eligibility.

While an escort is detached, its saved automatic assignment also keeps the original owned convoy eligible for threat response. A surviving escort in PURSUING, RETURNING or REFUELING preserves this relationship through save/load. The convoy independently assesses current shared sensor contacts and actual nearby own support; a distant escort contributes no local combat strength and need not arrive before evacuation. Each physically separate group must fund its own escape. The existing supply, passenger, trade and explicit-order gates still apply. Removed, empty or foreign escort fleets and SUPPLYING assignments do not authorize this response.

A convoy evacuation keeps its fleet ID, ships and cargo without creating a synthetic guard or escort order. The detached escort retains the convoy ID and its own saved identity. Later return uses the convoy's actual current site or supported moving course through ordinary funded travel and verified physical contact. A moving refuge retains its circular phase. Cross-system return and unsupported motion retain the existing waiting behavior.

The engine commits the selected preview on its simulation tick before ordinary interception, power and movement. Shared-sensor revocation, lost hardware or peace cancels onward pursuit through existing live-contact checks while preserving the physical journey and fuel already spent. Automatic defensive pursuit also reassesses currently detected enemies and immediately available own support each tick in supported motion. Enemy reinforcements or friendly damage can make the force overwhelming. A funded coordinated withdrawal then replaces pursuit with escape-return intent while retaining the original guard or convoy assignment. Explicit player intercept orders retain precedence.

## Coordinated escape

Campaign combat now invokes coordinated escape between simultaneous rounds using the surviving fleets' actual damage and remaining electrical stores. The same current sensor view and nearby own support determine whether danger is overwhelming. Combat ends early only after a complete group departure passes the existing physical, fuel, power and passenger gates. A blocked attempt keeps fighting with the state already paid by the completed round.

Explicit pursuit can be suspended during combat with its original mission saved for return; ordinary pre-combat order precedence is unchanged. Unassigned supporting fleets also retain their original stance, escort membership and pursuit through the existing saved intent record. Existing guard and detached escort recovery identities remain intact. Subsequent safe return reuses compatible fuel sharing, underfueled splits, base or tanker recovery and physical restoration. No new save field is required.

FleetProtectionProcessor considers up to eight common destinations among owned operational stations and same-body parking altitudes. A body-orbiting group considers stations around that same body. Candidate endpoints must increase separation from every assessed threat by at least 1,000 km and cannot be occupied by another detected wartime enemy. It prefers greater minimum separation with stable ordering. An itinerary requiring a future launch window cannot serve as an immediate escape. This is an escape attempt based on current contacts, not a guarantee against an enemy's future pursuit or an unseen threat.

Every own stationary fleet at the protected site must fund its leg to the same destination before anyone departs. Ordinary local or orbital planning, main fuel reserves, reactor feed, electrical forecasts and booked passenger supplies still apply. Supply orders, explicit intercepts and trade commitments that cannot be paused block automatic evacuation. A rejected leg yields ESCAPE_BLOCKED with no movement, fuel spending or order changes. Escorts remain joined to their protected convoy. A departing site guard relinquishes its active fixed-site order but saves the original owned object and physical site as escape-return intent. Each valid guard in the coordinated group retains its own assignment; unassigned support does not acquire a guard order.

After reaching a supported stationary or recovered circular refuge, FleetEscapeReturn requires no live or recent last-known wartime threat near either the refuge or the original guard site. Even a weaker remaining contact delays automatic return. Live detection uses the existing 100,000 km threat radius and actual shared hardware. Remembered observations use that radius enlarged by their position uncertainty until three-day expiry. The home-site check does not relocate a sensor or inspect hidden enemy motion. This conservative gate uses available observations and cannot guarantee the absence of unseen enemies or future pursuit. See [SensorModules.md](SensorModules.md#last-known-contacts).

The saved object must still be owned and valid before recovery purchases, return departure or restoration. Lost ownership, removal or a guarded fleet leaving its original site clears return intent without changing physical motion or refunding fuel. When clear, the guard reuses funded local return, compatible sharing, underfueled fleet splitting, paid base refueling and owned transport recovery. Internal sharing also works among ships in the same recovered circular fleet without changing its position, velocity or flight record; separate coasting fleets do not gain remote access. The guard resumes its order only after reaching the original site. An explicit replacement command can supersede saved return intent.

Save version 56 adds the escape-return safety marker to FleetProtectionAssignment and preserves it through return and refueling stages. Older records without the marker retain ordinary pursuit-return behavior. GuardEscapeReturnTest covers paid escape and return through saves, continued danger, a quiet refuge with a known threat at home, lost ownership, conserved sharing on an actual recovered coast, unavailable fuel, explicit stand-down and older-record compatibility.

Saved automatic guard and escort assignments in PURSUING, RETURNING or REFUELING participate in threat assessment while waiting or traveling. An overwhelming detected threat can trigger a coordinated funded escape from actual motion. The original object, convoy, home fleet identity and return site remain saved while obsolete refueling targets are cleared. Explicit intercept orders and transport supply assignments retain their existing precedence, including when present in nearby own support. A blocked escape leaves orders, trajectory and stores unchanged; automatic pursuit continues on its existing paid course until a withdrawal becomes feasible or ordinary pursuit eligibility ends.

If the remaining paid course already leads toward safety, continuation retains the exact trajectory and stores while marking the assignment as escape return. Recovery and restoration then wait for the existing quiet-refuge and quiet-home checks. When danger clears, ordinary fuel sharing, splitting, refueling and physical reattachment resume. Save version 56 already preserves these fields.

A body guard can resume on an actual recovered circular orbit at its saved radius without discarding position, velocity or phase. The body frame must agree, radius must match within one millimeter and circular velocity within 0.001 m/s. Station and fleet guards retain their physical contact requirements.

All departures commit on the simulation tick and then use the ordinary paid power and movement processors. Fleets may have different arrival times but share the destination. Saving preserves travel and the original guard's return intent. Cancellation or a later power failure retains ordinary physical motion and spent fuel. Unsupported drift waits for funded recovery; surface evacuation and cross-system escape are outside this candidate planner.

## Moving convoy responses

MovingFleetEscape checks a supported convoy's remaining committed route first. A forecast that increases separation from every currently assessed enemy by at least 1,000 km can yield CONTINUE_TO_SAFETY. Remaining main fuel, protected reserves, electricity and booked passenger supplies must still fund the route, and a station refuge must still be operational. Native coasts predict through their next circularization or capture endpoint. Enemy forecasts extrapolate observed ballistic motion rather than assuming an unseen future order. Unprojectable frames cannot authorize continuation as a safe route. This heuristic compares endpoints and does not guarantee avoidance of an enemy's later pursuit.

If continuation fails, the planner interrupts thrust on a temporary immutable copy and searches for a paid alternative. Planetary and lunar approaches reuse the shared gravity shooting solver from the exact current vector or native ellipse position and velocity. They may approach an owned same-body station or a circular parking orbit. Gravity-free local vectors and scalar flights use the existing finite-burn vector solver for an owned deep-space station. Every new trajectory must improve predicted separation from each assessed enemy at its own completion time. No gravity-center transition is invented. Stellar course changes and interstellar convoys remain unsupported by this escape search, although supported stellar native coasts can be assessed for continuation.

The group commits only if every member can fund an improving approach. The new trajectory begins at the actual saved position and velocity. Main fuel already spent is retained as spent, previously consumed reactor feed is not refunded and only the new leg's feed commitment is deducted at staging. Actual future burns consume main fuel through the ordinary power and movement processors. A blocked alternative leaves the original thrust, motion, stores and escort membership unchanged. Lost contact or diplomacy changes stop further threat-driven replanning without undoing a committed flight. Body parking endpoints retain physical circular motion rather than teleporting to a representative site phase.

## Trader delivery suspension

An escorted trader carrying a paid delivery or returning from one can suspend its trade route when the entire convoy commits to a funded escape. All assigned freighters must belong to that convoy. Loading and repositioning commitments remain blocking. PAUSED_DELIVERY and PAUSED_RETURN retain the endpoints, active flag, mixed cargo manifest, paid cost basis and operating results through saves. A blocked escape or continuation along the existing safer route leaves the trade route unchanged. The engine retains the protection processor's route changes before logistics runs on the same tick.

Logistics makes no freight purchase or sale during suspension. It reconciles the manifest against cargo actually remaining aboard and waits for stationary arrival or completed circular body recovery. An overwhelming detected threat still blocks resumption. Once safe, an active route first attempts a funded journey to its original operational buyer without purchasing its cargo again. An unavailable buyer, canceled route or unfundable delivery instead permits a funded return to a safe source. Known wartime enemies within the local threat radius make a port unsuitable. A rendezvous that is currently unfundable is retried on later ticks while the trader retains its cargo.

RETURNING_CARGO holds paid goods at the source without an automatic refund, sale or replacement purchase. An active route may resume the original delivery when it becomes viable. Only the ordinary delivery settlement records a sale and moved volume. Paid refueling and port electrical upkeep remain ordinary operating expenses. If neither destination is viable, the load remains aboard. A completed circular recovery can resume travel around the same body; cross-system resumption uses the existing departure path after stationary arrival.

## Repair recovery decisions

Returning automatic guards and detached escorts assess known living owned ships before restoring their protection assignment. Any damage to installed repairable hardware or hull below 80 percent of the current 1,000-point commissioning limit triggers repair consideration. The hull threshold is provisional. Hardware must be restored or the hull threshold reached before a useful repair wait ends; full hull restoration is not required for return.

With no live local wartime contact, a bounded preview of one actual power and onboard repair tick takes precedence when it improves the repair need. Otherwise accessible same-system powered and staffed base workshops are considered by current distance then station ID. A detected wartime enemy within the ordinary threat radius of a base excludes it. Existing overwhelming-threat escape assessment retains precedence.

A base detour requires funded outbound travel, useful paid daily repair and funded onward return or rendezvous after projected local paid refueling. The repair quote uses the actual station-wide work order and competing docked fleets. It checks current stock and cash rather than reserving future availability. Forecast repairs, purchases, refills and service receipts do not mutate the live state. Departure commits ordinary motion and fuel; the normal repair tick commits actual work and payment.

REPAIRING saves the base station ID or an empty target for onboard work together with the original guard, convoy, home fleet and escape provenance. Travel must finish through ordinary movement. Every available recovery tick rechecks useful work, stock, cash, staffing, power, access and onward funding. No useful repair option releases the fleet to its existing funded return and fuel recovery rules with actual damage and spent resources intact. Supply duties are not rerouted. A completed repair restores a guard at its valid site or an escort through actual physical transfer. Ordinary order repair below also supports an available explicit interception before departure.

Save version 58 adds the stage without a separate repair queue. Older saves retain their existing protection stages. Cross-system recovery and unsupported moving rendezvous still wait under the existing travel rules. Repair preview work is bounded to one daily tick per candidate and introduces no long balance simulation.

### Ordinary fleet orders

The common fleet order tick also considers ordinary idle space fleets owned by players, AI empires and corporations. Known owned designs, surviving ships and useful funded repair are required. The same hardware and provisional hull threshold apply. Useful onboard work takes precedence over a safe accessible base and rejected choices preserve the original state.

FleetRepairIntent retains the previous stance, guard, joined escort membership or explicit interception alongside the original site. The engine temporarily clears live assignments only after accepting a useful repair choice. A convoy repairs as one physical fleet without inventing an escort detachment. Completed or exhausted service uses ordinary return travel and fuel recovery, then restores the previous assignment at the original site. An interception resumes only if the original target is still a currently detected same-system wartime contact and the fleet still qualifies for military interception. Lost contact or peace clears that saved pursuit. Guard ownership and object validity remain required.

Existing local and interstellar journeys finish physically before this selection can pause a fleet. Surface fleets, passenger bookings, pending supply deliveries, active rescue participants and active or unresolved trade routes retain their commitments. Idle ships with paid cargo retain the cargo during repairs. Ordinary explicit pursuit can pause before departure when no local wartime threat prevents repair; committed pursuit motion continues. Automatic repair recovery remains subject to the existing overwhelming-threat escape response and keeps its saved intent through withdrawal.

Guard and interception cancellations remove the saved order as well as its eventual restoration. Escort detachment uses the normal split while preserving both fragments' paid itinerary, progress and actual ship condition. A new stance or replacement order supersedes saved intent. Automatic repair consideration can still occur afterward under the new assignment. The frontend shows paused guard and escort status plus repair recovery status and stages all cancellations through commands.

Save version 59 adds nullable FleetRepairIntent to the existing protection assignment. Versions without it retain the previous recovery behavior and do not acquire an invented assignment. Configurable per-fleet repair policy, mid-flight mission suspension and surface repair routing remain future mechanics.
